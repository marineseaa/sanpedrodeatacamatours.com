The town sits at about 2,400 metres, and many of the main trips climb past 4,000 metres, where summer storms can affect the roads even when San Pedro itself is dry. If you travel over Christmas, book your room and the departures you care about earlier than usual.

You get red rock that glows late into the evening in Valle de la Luna, flamingos feeding in the salt-flat lagoons, and Andean slopes that can turn green after summer rain. The trade-off is that each elevation needs its own plan: a T-shirt for lunch in town will not keep you warm before dawn at El Tatio.

The main points for a December trip:

- **Season:** early southern-hemisphere summer, with the longest days of the year.
- **Town weather:** usually hot and dry by day, much cooler after dark. Average highs are about 32 °C and lows about 7 °C.
- **Highland weather:** cold at dawn and exposed; storms of the Altiplanic winter can begin late in December.
- **Easiest outing:** Valle de la Luna, if the sector and route you booked are open.
- **Biggest scenery day:** Piedras Rojas and the Miscanti and Miñiques lagoons, once you have acclimatised and the road is open.
- **Most memorable early start:** El Tatio geysers at sunrise, also subject to weather and altitude.
- **Darkest nights in December 2026:** around the new Moon on 8 December; choose the session by moonrise, moonset and the forecast.
- **Holidays:** more demand towards Christmas and New Year; don't assume normal opening hours on holiday dates.

## Is December a good month to visit San Pedro de Atacama?

For a first trip, yes, as long as you plan around altitude and the chance of highland weather delays. December brings good sightseeing in the lower desert, long daylight for photography and plenty of tours to choose from. If you have only two days and one high-altitude trip you cannot miss, a shoulder month with steadier weather is easier to plan.

December suits you if you:

- Want to combine San Pedro with a summer trip through Chile.
- Like long sightseeing days and don't mind carrying both sun protection and cold-weather layers.
- Can spend at least four nights and keep one tour slot flexible.
- Want a Christmas or New Year trip and can reserve key services in advance.

Consider another month if you:

- Need the best possible chance of completing a specific highland route on one fixed day.
- Dislike intense sun, dust and hot afternoons.
- Are travelling only for faint-object astronomy during Christmas week, when the Moon is bright in 2026.

For month-by-month alternatives, see [Best Time to Visit San Pedro de Atacama](/best-time-to-visit/).

## December weather: hot town, cold mountains

Town, salt flats and Andean plateau each have their own weather, so check the one for where you will be.

| Where you'll be | What December is like | What to bring | What can disrupt the plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| **San Pedro town and nearby valleys** (about 2,400 m) | Strong sun and hot afternoons, with a big drop after sunset | Sun hat, sunscreen, water, breathable clothing, light jacket | Heat, strong wind, dust; unusual heavy rain can affect streets and access |
| **Valle de la Luna** (about 2,500 m) | Exposed sun and reflective rock; cooler evenings, possible wind | Closed shoes, water, sunglasses, wind layer | Reserve or sector closures, unsafe conditions |
| **Rainbow Valley / Puritama area** (about 3,200–3,500 m) | Noticeably cooler than town, especially in shade or wind | Fleece, windproof shell, sturdy footwear | Road and weather conditions |
| **Miscanti, Miñiques, Piedras Rojas** (about 4,000–4,200 m) | Thin air, intense UV, cold wind and possible afternoon cloud or storms | Warm layers, gloves, waterproof and windproof outer layer | Highland rain, storms and route closures |
| **El Tatio** (about 4,300 m) | Very cold before sunrise even in summer | Thermal layer, insulated jacket, hat, gloves, warm socks | Dawn cold, road conditions, storms and altitude |

According to the station figures on our [best-time guide](/best-time-to-visit/), San Pedro has a December average high of about 32 °C and an average low of about 7 °C, with under 1 mm of rain. Temperatures vary from year to year, so use these for packing, not as a forecast for your dates. With the altitude range, you can have a freezing morning and a hot afternoon on the same trip.

**How to check the forecast:** look at the town forecast for evenings and casual outings, then ask the operator separately about the named highland route you booked. A clear forecast in San Pedro does not mean the roads near Socaire or El Tatio are passable. Check again the afternoon before departure.

### What is the Altiplanic winter, and does it start in December?

It can. *Invierno altiplánico* translates as "Altiplanic winter", but the name refers to a summer rainy-season pattern in the high Andes, not Chile's June–August winter. From about late December to March, moist air can reach the plateau and bring cloud, showers or storms. Rain does not fall every day and its effects are uneven: the town can be bright and dry while a higher road is unsafe.

Chile's official tourism board warns that highland rain and storms in this period can close routes. Ask yourself whether your booked trip can reach its stops safely, not only whether it will rain in San Pedro.

If plans change, don't improvise a remote highland drive yourself. Follow the registered operator's advice, obey closures and ask about rebooking before you agree to a substitute itinerary.

## Stargazing in December 2026: choose the Moon, not just the month

The Atacama is famous for astronomy, but a dark sky is not guaranteed just because you are in the desert. Cloud, moonlight, artificial light and the operator's cancellation policy all matter.

Moon phases for San Pedro in December 2026 (local time):

| Date | Moon phase | What it means for a night-sky tour |
|---|---|---|
| **1 December** | Third quarter | The Moon rises around midnight, so the early evening is dark |
| **8 December** | **New Moon** | The best start point for faint stars and Milky Way photos, if the sky is clear |
| **17 December** | First quarter | The Moon lights the early evening; check when it sets |
| **23 December** | **Full Moon** | Poor for a dark-sky session, but the Moon itself is a fine telescope target if the operator runs one |
| **30 December** | Third quarter | The early evening is darker; confirm moonrise for your session |

The Moon phase does not guarantee that a tour runs or that the sky is clear. Some operators suspend astronomy tours on bright nights; others offer a Moon-focused session instead, so ask which kind you are buying. December's late sunsets also mean sessions start later than in winter, at about 21:00.

**What to ask an astronomy operator:**

1. Is my exact date a dark-sky, telescope or astrophotography session?
2. What happens if the Moon is bright or cloud comes in?
3. What does the price include: transport, guide, telescope viewing, photos?
4. Is it a small group or a large shared session?
5. How cold is the observing site likely to be, and what time will we be back?

If the sky is your main reason to travel, give yourself two possible evenings. ## Does San Pedro get busy in December?

Yes, especially around Christmas and New Year, though how busy depends on the date, the tour and the type of accommodation. We have no reliable price series showing that December is cheaper or quieter than January, so we don't claim it.

For late December, book these first:

- **Accommodation:** especially a central, walkable room, family space or a hotel with a pool.
- **The highland trip you most want:** keeping a second possible date matters in a month when weather can cancel a departure.
- **Stargazing on a dark night:** confirm the operator runs over the holidays.
- **Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve meals:** don't count on restaurants having normal service or walk-in tables.
- **Airport transfer:** especially for late arrivals, large families and travel between 24 December and 1 January.

Book lower-stakes outings more flexibly where the cancellation terms allow. Check each tour's inclusions: a headline price may exclude reserve entrance fees, and some official tickets are bought separately.

## A four-day December itinerary

This is a sample order of days, not a medical acclimatisation plan; people react to altitude differently. If you develop symptoms, rest and get medical advice before going any higher.

### Day 1: Arrive and find your bearings

Fly into Calama's El Loa Airport, transfer to San Pedro, eat, drink plenty of water and explore the small town on foot. If your flight is early and you feel well, consider a guided Valle de la Luna afternoon; otherwise keep the day simple. Don't book a 4,000-metre trip for the day after flying in from sea level.

### Day 2: Lower desert and a night-sky session

Choose Rainbow Valley and the Yerbas Buenas rock art, a lower lagoon or a gentle morning walk. Rest in the hottest part of the afternoon. Book stargazing only if the Moon, the cloud outlook and the operator's schedule suit what you want to see.

### Day 3: Piedras Rojas and the Altiplanic Lagoons

If you have adjusted well and the road is open, this is the big landscape day: an early start, a long drive, high stops and a late-afternoon return. Sort out tickets and any dietary needs before you go, and leave the evening free.

### Day 4: El Tatio at dawn, then a quiet afternoon

Go only if you feel well at altitude and the road is safe. You are back in town around late morning or midday, depending on the operator. Have lunch and rest, or book a Puritama visit if you have the energy. If a safer weather window opens for one of the two highland trips, swap days 3 and 4.

**Five nights are better.** A fifth night gives you room to move a highland trip after a closure, try a second night of stargazing and enjoy San Pedro without spending the whole visit in a vehicle. Our [three-day itinerary](/3-day-itinerary/) shows a shorter version.

## What to pack for San Pedro de Atacama in December

Pack for two seasons and several elevations, not a single summer temperature.

**For town and daytime trips:** breathable tops, trousers or shorts, a brimmed hat, high-SPF sunscreen, lip balm, sunglasses, a refillable water bottle and closed walking shoes. UV is strong even when the air feels cool.

**For the geysers and high lagoons:** a thermal base layer, fleece, insulated windproof jacket, warm socks, gloves and a beanie. A packable waterproof shell helps in mountain weather but does not replace insulation.

**For stargazing:** long trousers, warm layers, closed shoes and a hat. You spend much of the session standing still outdoors after dark.

**For lagoons or hot springs:** swimwear and a towel only where your venue allows bathing, plus dry clothes and a bag for wet things.

**Practical extras:** some Chilean pesos in cash for places that don't take cards, a charged phone with offline maps, a power bank, any prescription medicine and the documents you need for onward border crossings. Summer dresses and sandals alone won't do, even in December.

## Altitude and health in December

San Pedro is at about 2,400 metres, high enough for some travellers to get headaches or feel short of breath. El Tatio and the Altiplanic Lagoons are above 4,000 metres. A day or two in town does not guarantee you have acclimatised for them.

Avoid hard exertion right after arrival, drink water normally, don't rely on alcohol to help you sleep and pay attention to symptoms. If you get a headache with nausea, dizziness or unusual tiredness after going up, don't go any higher and tell your guide. Severe breathlessness at rest, confusion, loss of coordination or quickly worsening symptoms are emergencies: descend promptly and get medical help. If you have heart, lung or pregnancy concerns, get personal medical advice before booking a very high trip.

Choose an operator that takes health restrictions seriously. Our [altitude guide](/altitude/) has more detail.

## December mistakes to avoid in San Pedro de Atacama

1. **Thinking December is winter.** In Chile it is early summer; the "Altiplanic winter" is a summer rain pattern in the highlands.
2. **Trusting the town forecast for a 4,000-metre trip.** Weather and road status differ sharply with elevation.
3. **Booking El Tatio for your first morning.** The earliest free slot is not always the best one for your body.
4. **Buying a stargazing tour without checking the Moon.** The full Moon on 23 December 2026 matters most to Christmas visitors.
5. **Assuming every salt lagoon allows swimming.** Access and bathing permissions change by place and date.
6. **Reading a tour title as the inclusion list.** Confirm named stops, meals, admission, cancellation and pickup.
7. **Expecting Christmas Day or New Year's Day to run like a normal weekday.** Check restaurants, tours and transfers one by one.
8. **Leaving no spare day.** One weather-hit day should not cost you the only highland trip you came to Chile to see.

## Planning your December trip

In December, treat the desert as several climates: hot lower valleys, freezing geyser fields at dawn and highlands that can catch summer storms. With four or five nights, a gradual climb, checked entrance tickets and a stargazing night chosen by the Moon, you can see the main Atacama landscapes without needing perfect weather every day.
