San Pedro de Atacama is a village of adobe walls and dirt streets, but it has one of northern Chile's most interesting food scenes. Here you can order a pisco sour flavoured with **rica-rica**, a minty herb that grows in the high Andes. You can follow it with **chañar** ice cream made from a desert fruit and a glass of **Ayllu**, a wine made by a cooperative of Lickanantay (Atacameño) growers from high-altitude vineyards around Toconao. Then you eat beside an open-air bonfire while an Andean band plays and the Milky Way comes out overhead.

Eating out is not cheap here, because almost everything is trucked in across the desert. Service can be slow, and the whole town winds down early. This guide covers where to pay more, where to save and what to order.

## Our picks: short reviews

### Adobe: best atmosphere in San Pedro de Atacama

Adobe opened in August 1997 and is still the town's default first-night dinner. The roof is partly open, there is a bonfire in the middle, and Andean bands play most evenings. The walls are covered in ceramics by the artist Tatané Durán. The menu runs from quinoa, arrope and patasca to pizza and lomo a lo pobre, so it suits mixed groups and children. It has thousands of TripAdvisor reviews, and some of them call it pricey and the service slow, which is why it is not our pick for food. Bring a jacket, because it gets cold under the open roof, and sit away from the smoke.

### Ckunna: best all-round dinner

Ckunna is in a restored adobe house with a large courtyard and a fire pit. It mixes Andean ingredients with Italian technique, so expect homemade pasta, carpaccio and meat with chañar sauce. Reviewers regularly say it pours one of the best pisco sours in Chile, and it scores higher than Adobe on value. Listings disagree on which days it opens (TripAdvisor shows Tuesday to Saturday), so check its own website before you walk over.

### Rai: best newer restaurant

Rai is the restaurant at {{< hotel-link "desertica" >}}Hotel Desertica{{< /hotel-link >}} (Caracoles 455) and takes non-guests for lunch and dinner. It serves contemporary Chilean food using ingredients from the Altiplano, the desert and the Pacific, with dishes finished with chañar, rica-rica and algarrobo. The room is quiet and well lit. In October 2026 it had one of the highest TripAdvisor scores in town, from fewer than 100 reviews: a small sample, but a consistent one.

### Baltinache restaurant: best Atacameño cooking (if it's open)

The Baltinache restaurant in town (not the Baltinache lagoons) is a small, family-run room where the chefs combine Atacameño and Mapuche cooking. The set menu has featured patasca, fish in rica-rica sauce, guanaco, algarrobo-seed ice cream and chañar mousse. Bookings have been by WhatsApp, with two evening seatings. Some Google-based listings show it as temporarily closed, although diners reviewed it into late 2025, so message it before you plan an evening around it and keep Rai as your fallback.

### La Picada del Indio: best value

La Picada del Indio is on Tocopilla, one block off the main street. It serves a three-course **menú del día** at lunch and dinner, which reviews in 2025–26 put at about CLP 8,000. Older reviews quote CLP 4,500–5,500, so prices have risen fast. It has vegetarian options, cocktail happy hours and a lively patio. Drinks cost about CLP 5,000 each in recent reviews and can double your bill.

### La Casona: best traditional Chilean

La Casona is a big colonial house on Caracoles with a fireplace and live music. Go here for pastel de choclo, cazuela and empanadas. It is also one of the few places with real vegan versions of Chilean classics, including vegan pastel de choclo, vegan chorrillana and mushroom pil-pil. Reviews are mixed, so expect dependable Chilean cooking without surprises.

### Emporio Andino: best empanadas and coffee

Emporio Andino is a small café that turns out baked empanadas. Order pino (beef), goat cheese with basil, or the vegan "Red" and "Green" fillings. Reviewers also rate its espresso among the best in town. Buy a few the night before an early tour.

### Heladería Babalú: best ice cream

This is the place to taste the desert as ice cream. Flavours include rica-rica, chañar, algarrobo, quinoa, coca leaf and ayrampo (a cactus). Recent reviews quote about CLP 3,500 for a single scoop and CLP 5,000 for a double, and staff let you taste before you choose. It has several branches on Caracoles.

### Rincón de Sal: best casual dinner

Rincón de Sal has some of the best-rated burgers and pizza in town on TripAdvisor. It opens early on some days and does takeaway, so it is the easiest option after a sunset tour such as the {{< gyg-link tour="466455" >}}Valle de la Luna shared tour (from US$32, 4.7/5 from 366 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}}.

## Tipping, payment and service

- **Tipping:** Chilean law (article 64 of the Labour Code, amended in 2014) requires restaurants, bars and cafés to suggest a tip of at least 10% on the bill. The tip itself is voluntary, so a restaurant cannot add it without asking. The waiter will ask "¿Desea agregar la propina?" ("Would you like to add the tip?"). Leaving the 10% is the norm.
- **Payment:** cards are widely accepted in town. Carry cash for Machuca, small stalls and village stops.
- **IVA (VAT):** menu prices already include it.
- **Service pace:** slow by international standards, especially on Caracoles in peak season. Allow an extra 30 minutes at dinner if you have a stargazing pickup.
- **Reservations:** book small places a day ahead in peak season (December to February, July, Easter and the Chilean holidays in September). The Baltinache restaurant has taken bookings by WhatsApp only. Hotel restaurants ask non-guests to book.

## Opening hours and timing

- **Lunch** runs from about 12:30 to 15:30, and that is when set menus are served. Many kitchens close around 15:30–16:00 and reopen for dinner around 18:00–19:30.
- **Dinner** is usually from about 19:30 to 22:30, and many kitchens stop taking orders between 22:00 and 23:00.
- **Early tour days:** El Tatio departures are around 04:30–05:10 and most tours include breakfast; the {{< gyg-link tour="345946" >}}El Tatio sunrise tour with breakfast (from US$73, 4.7/5 from 1,151 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} also stops at Machuca, where stalls sell llama anticuchos. Buy empanadas the night before. Our [El Tatio geysers guide](/el-tatio-geysers-tours/) compares the departures.
- **Day off:** several restaurants close one weekday, often Monday or Tuesday.

## Nightlife: best things to do at night in San Pedro de Atacama

Night-time here means stargazing, a long fire-pit dinner and one or two drinks. There are no nightclubs.

- **Why it's quiet:** in 2022 BioBioChile reported that a municipal ordinance stops dancing in night venues. There has been nowhere to dance since 2000, when the town's only dance venue, on the corner of Caracoles and Toconao, closed. A resident quoted in that report said the centre closes at about 1 a.m. from Sunday to Thursday and 2 a.m. on Friday and Saturday. Treat those hours as a guide and check locally, because the municipality sets them under its ordinance on alcohol licences and opening hours.
- **Alcohol with food:** many licensed restaurants serve alcohol only with a meal. That is why travellers keep asking whether they "have to order chips with a beer". **Chela Cabur** is one of the few places where you can just drink.
- **Top night-time options:**
  1. **Stargazing tour.** The town's main night out. The {{< gyg-link tour="209091" >}}Stargazing & Andean Astronomy with Wine tour (from about US$30, 4.7/5 from 798 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} adds a glass of wine to the telescope session, and the {{< gyg-link tour="542449" >}}llama stargazing session (from US$30, 4.7/5 from 814 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} is the alternative if you would rather meet the llamas. Our [stargazing tours guide](/stargazing-tours/) compares them.
  2. **Fire-pit dinner with live Andean music** at Adobe, Ckunna, La Casona or Caracol Bar.
  3. **Happy hour** on Tocopilla (La Picada del Indio) or at Rai.
  4. **Craft beer** at Chela Cabur or St. Peter.
  5. **A night walk to the plaza** to look at the sky. The town keeps its lighting low.
- **Getting home safely:** streets outside the centre are dark and unpaved, so carry a headlamp. Packs of street dogs are common; walk in company and do not feed them. Do not drink in public. You are at about 2,400 m, where alcohol hits harder; see the [altitude guide](/altitude/).

## Vegetarian, vegan and gluten-free in San Pedro de Atacama

- **Fully vegan or vegetarian:** Estrella Negra on Caracoles (organic, plant-based).
- **Strong vegan options:** La Casona (vegan pastel de choclo, chorrillana, mushroom pil-pil), Karavan (vegan ceviche, falafel, lentil burger), Emporio Andino (vegan empanadas), Tierra Todo Natural (vegetarian section and juices), Ckunna and Adobe (marked options).
- **Vegetarian set menus:** La Picada del Indio and Sol Inti usually have a meat-free menú del día.
- **Babalú** has dairy-free sorbets.
- **Gluten-free:** TripAdvisor lists gluten-free options at Adobe and Ckunna. Coeliac awareness is moderate, so say "*soy celíaco/a, sin gluten*" (I'm coeliac, gluten-free).
- **Heads-up:** the Machuca stop on El Tatio tours sells mostly llama meat and cheese empanadas, so bring your own snack if you are vegan.

## Self-catering, water and food safety

- **Supermarkets:** there are only small minimarkets, and prices are higher than in Calama. If you are self-catering for several days, stock up in Calama on the way in; the [Calama airport transfer guide](/calama-airport-to-san-pedro-de-atacama/) covers the drive.
- **Water:** tap water is very mineral-heavy, and most visitors drink filtered or bottled water. Many hotels have refill stations, so bring a bottle and drink 2–3 litres a day at altitude.
- **Food safety:** restaurants here have a good record. Ceviche is fine at busy places. At village stalls, choose cooked food.

## Food experiences beyond restaurants

- **Ayllu winery, Toconao (about 40 km south):** the Lickanantay high-altitude winegrowers' cooperative makes Ayllu wine from vineyards around Toconao. Its winery tours are sold through Enoticket: the basic visit with three wines starts at about CLP 25,000, and longer tours add a vineyard walk. Transport is not included, so combine it with a Salar de Atacama or lagoons day; our [tour comparison](/tours/) lists those trips.
- **Culinary walking tour:** Nomades lists a walking food tour from the town plaza, with tastings of local infusions with chañar syrup, Rica Rica Ale and a rica-rica pisco sour. Check its current price when you book.
- **Machuca village:** the stop on the way back from El Tatio on the {{< gyg-link tour="345946" >}}El Tatio sunrise tour with breakfast (from US$73, 4.7/5 from 1,151 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}}, with llama anticuchos and goat-cheese empanadas (cash only).
- **Fiestas:** the Toconao Vendimia (harvest festival) and village carnivals are the best chance to eat patasca the traditional way.

## Fine dining and Michelin Keys

Chile has no Michelin restaurant guide, so no San Pedro restaurant holds a Michelin star. Michelin covers Chile through its hotel Keys instead. {{< hotel-link "tierra_atacama" >}}Tierra Atacama{{< /hotel-link >}} moved up to two Keys in the 2026 selection, and {{< hotel-link "awasi" >}}Awasi Atacama{{< /hotel-link >}} and {{< hotel-link "nayara" >}}Nayara Alto Atacama{{< /hotel-link >}} were among the two-Key hotels in 2025. A Key is a hotel award, not a restaurant rating. For a hotel dinner as a non-guest, Rai at Hotel Desertica and Kunza at {{< hotel-link "cumbres" >}}Hotel Cumbres{{< /hotel-link >}} both take bookings; our [where to stay guide](/where-to-stay/) covers the hotels themselves.

## Common complaints and how to avoid them

| Complaint | Fix |
|---|---|
| "Everything is expensive" | Eat your main meal at lunch with a menú del día. Use the Tocopilla Street spots. Share mains, because portions are large. |
| "Service is slow" | Do not book dinner right before a stargazing pickup. Order everything at once. |
| "Touristy Caracoles" | Rai, Ckunna, La Picada del Indio and Sol Inti are just off the main street or at its quiet end. |
| "Smoky clothes" | Fire-pit restaurants are smoky, so ask for a table upwind. |
| "Couldn't get just a beer" | Go to Chela Cabur, or order a snack. |
| "Closed when we arrived" | Many places close mid-afternoon and one weekday. Check Google Maps on the day. |

Opening days and prices change often in a small town, so check each place on the day you plan to go, and book a stargazing tour for after dinner on your clearest night.
