## Build a shortlist with variety

For a first visit, our core comparisons are [Piedras Rojas and the Altiplanic Lagoons](/piedras-rojas-tours/), [El Tatio](/el-tatio-geysers-tours/), [stargazing](/stargazing-tours/) and [Moon Valley](/moon-valley-tours/), with a floating afternoon at [Laguna Cejar](/laguna-cejar-tours/). You do not need all of them if your time or altitude response makes the schedule too demanding.

San Pedro is an adobe village at about 2,400 m. Almost everything you will want to see lies 15 minutes to 2.5 hours away by van, often on gravel roads and often much higher than the town. Picking tours here comes down to altitude, start times and entry tickets that now have to be bought online in advance.

## Go low before you go high

San Pedro is at about 2,400 m, and the big tours climb to 4,000–4,300 m within 1.5–2.5 hours. The Cejar site advises spending at least one day in San Pedro first, and operators commonly suggest leaving the lagoons and Rainbow Valley for later in your stay. The [altitude guide](/altitude/) covers symptoms and when to see a doctor.

| Step | Altitude | Tours |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (arrival day) | 2,300–2,500 m | Valle de la Luna, Cejar and Piedra, Valle de la Muerte, Quitor and Tulor by bike |
| 2 | 3,200–3,600 m | Rainbow Valley and Yerbas Buenas, Puritama |
| 3 | 4,000–4,200 m | Altiplanic Lagoons and Piedras Rojas |
| 4 | Above 4,300 m | El Tatio at sunrise |
| 5 (optional) | 4,300–5,600 m | Salar de Tara, Cerro Toco, Láscar, the Uyuni crossing |

Scheduling clashes to avoid:

- **No stargazing the night before El Tatio.** Stargazing can finish around midnight, and the El Tatio pickup is at 4:30–5:10 a.m.
- **Leave a gap between an afternoon tour and stargazing.** Afternoon tours such as Cejar return at 18:30–19:30, and operators advise at least 45 minutes before a stargazing start.
- **Pair El Tatio with Puritama in the afternoon, or a nap,** not with another 4,000 m day.

### Plans by trip length

- **2 days:** Cejar and Piedra or Valle de la Luna in the afternoon, then stargazing; on day 2, the Altiplanic Lagoons and Piedras Rojas. Skip El Tatio unless you are already acclimatised from Bolivia or Peru. Full plan: [2-day itinerary](/2-day-itinerary/).
- **3 days:** Valle de la Luna and stargazing; Rainbow Valley in the morning and Cejar in the afternoon; the Altiplanic Lagoons on day 3, with El Tatio as a fourth morning if your flight allows. Full plan: [3-day itinerary](/3-day-itinerary/).
- **4 days (our recommendation):** Valle de la Luna; Rainbow Valley, Cejar and stargazing; the lagoons with Chaxa; El Tatio, then Puritama or rest. Full plan: [4-day itinerary](/4-day-itinerary/).
- **5 days:** the four-day plan with stargazing on night 1 or 3, then Salar de Tara, Cerro Toco for fit hikers, or sandboarding and biking at lower altitude.

## Tour by tour

### Valle de la Luna (Moon Valley)

Highlights are Duna Mayor, the Tres Marías salt formations, the Amphitheatre, the old Victoria salt mine and the Cornisa ledges. General entry was CLP 10,800 when we checked in October 2026, bought online for a set date. The gate opens at 9 a.m. from September to March and 8 a.m. from April to August, closing to cars at 5 p.m. and 4 p.m. respectively; you cannot enter on foot, and cyclists have a morning-only slot.

The official site says the Mirador de Ckari viewpoint has been closed until further notice since 27 May 2026 for a post-earthquake safety evaluation, and it lists staff-training closures in October, the last on 27 October. A normal car can make the 13 km drive. Compare tours in the [Moon Valley guide](/moon-valley-tours/).

### Laguna Cejar and Laguna Piedra

Cejar is a flamingo-and-salt-crust wetland under Licancabur, for viewing only; you float at Laguna Piedra next door. Tours continue to the Ojos del Salar freshwater pools and end at sunset on the Tebinquiche salt mirror. One online ticket covers Cejar and Piedra: CLP 15,000 for ages 13–59, CLP 10,000 for 60 and over and CLP 5,000 up to 12, valid for one hour from your booked time (official site, checked October 2026). Apply no sunscreen before swimming, stay in the water no more than about 20 minutes, and bring your own water, as nothing is sold on site. Details in the [Laguna Cejar guide](/laguna-cejar-tours/).

### Baltinache hidden lagoons

Seven turquoise salt lagoons in the Cordillera de la Sal, about an hour away on gravel and managed with the Coyo community. Bathing and floating have been suspended since 29 September 2026; the site stays open for walking at a reduced cash fee. SanPedroAtacama.com, a local operator, expects one lagoon to reopen for floating in December, but there is no confirmed official date. See the [Baltinache guide](/baltinache-tours/) and the [Baltinache vs Cejar comparison](/baltinache-vs-laguna-cejar/).

### Rainbow Valley and Yerbas Buenas petroglyphs

You see red, green, white and yellow mineral hills, dry canyons and hundreds of pre-Columbian rock carvings of llamas, foxes and more, about 90 km from town at 3,200–3,600 m. Tours run roughly 07:00–13:00, with two small fees paid in cash. With a car it is about an hour each way. Book the {{< viator-link path="San-Pedro-de-Atacama/Rainbow-Valley-Tour-from-San-Pedro-de-Atacama/d5499-6315CJCRAIN" >}}Rainbow Valley Tour on Viator (4.7/5 from 35 reviews){{< /viator-link >}}.

### Puritama hot springs

Natural pools in a green canyon linked by boardwalks, 30 km north of town and run by Explora. The official site lists two entry blocks, 09:30–13:30 and 14:30–18:00, every day, with a child rate under 12 and free entry under 3. Book the entry on [termasdepuritama.cl](https://termasdepuritama.cl/en/shop/) and carry the ticket offline, as there is no signal. The only GetYourGuide listing, the {{< gyg-link tour="832294" >}}Puritama Hot Springs thermal bath transfer{{< /gyg-link >}}, has a single review so far. See the [Puritama guide](/puritama-hot-springs-tours/).

### Laguna Chaxa

Andean, Chilean and James's flamingos feeding on the Salar de Atacama, run by the Toconao community with tickets sold online. Sunset is the photographers' choice. Visitors without a ticket have had to buy one at the gate with almost no signal. Most people see it on the lagoons day, for example on the {{< gyg-link tour="632997" >}}Red stones, highland lagoons and Chaxa triple tour{{< /gyg-link >}}.

### Altiplanic Lagoons and Piedras Rojas

Deep-blue Miscanti and Miñiques at over 4,100 m, with vicuñas and foxes, then the rust-red rock of Piedras Rojas on the Salar de Aguas Calientes, about 150–160 km from town, with stops in Socaire and Toconao. The Socaire community sells tickets only online at socairechile.cl; none are sold at the parks, and you must check in at Socaire an hour before your visit with your QR code and vehicle plate. Daily numbers are capped. Wind and snow sometimes close Piedras Rojas on the day. Compare options in the [Piedras Rojas guide](/piedras-rojas-tours/).

### El Tatio geysers

More than 80 geysers and fumaroles above 4,300 m, at their steamiest around sunrise. A 2003 study by Glennon and Pfaff describes El Tatio as the largest geyser field in the southern hemisphere and the third largest in the world. On the way back, tours stop at the Putana river wetland and the adobe village of Machuca. The El Tatio fee page lists CLP 15,000 for everyone aged 9 and over, says under-9s are not admitted and lists the thermal pool as not open. Expect −5 °C to −10 °C at dawn, a pickup between 4:30 and 5:10, and stay on the marked paths: the thin crust near the vents can hide scalding water. The featured tour runs about six hours with breakfast. More in the [El Tatio guide](/el-tatio-geysers-tours/).

### Salar de Tara and Monjes de la Pacana

The route passes balanced ignimbrite "monk" pillars, the Quisquiro and Tara salt flats, and wetlands near the Chile–Argentina–Bolivia border, at about 4,300 m with passes over 4,800 m, so do it last. Agencies in town sell it; the online listings we found have only a handful of reviews, and one premium operator listed it as closed until further notice. CONAF and the Lickanantay community of Toconao closed the Salar de Tara itself to visitors in June 2018, and [Mongabay reported in 2023](https://es.mongabay.com/2023/05/salar-de-tara-industria-del-litio-chile/) that it was still closed, so ask exactly which stops a route reaches. Self-drivers report poor signposting; take a guide.

### Valle de la Muerte sandboarding

Dunes 2 km from town, with practice runs of about 40 m and descents of up to 200 m. The gate fee is paid by card, and Taito Aventura reports that the valley closes every Monday. Children can usually join from about 8. You walk back up after every run, which is hard work at 2,500 m. Book with an agency in town; we found no online listing to recommend.

### Pukará de Quitor, Aldea de Tulor and Garganta del Diablo {#pukara-de-quitor-aldea-de-tulor-and-garganta-del-diablo}

Pukará de Quitor is a 12th-century hilltop fortress 3 km from town, run by the Quitor community; some 2026 visitors found parts of it closed. Aldea de Tulor is an adobe village more than 2,000 years old, run by the Coyo community, with cash entry. Both are easy by bike, as is the half-day ride north through the narrow red canyon of Garganta del Diablo and Catarpe. Carry 2 litres of water and go in the morning, before the wind picks up, or join the {{< viator-link path="San-Pedro-de-Atacama/4-Hours-riding-a-bicycle-in-San-Pedro-de-Atacama/d5499-409609P1" >}}Devil's Throat bike tour (5.0/5 from 110 reviews){{< /viator-link >}}. Several agencies also offer horseback rides through the Catarpe and Quitor valleys, useful low-altitude outings for day 1.

### Volcano climbs

- **Cerro Toco (5,604 m):** about 7 hours, starting at about 5,000 m at an old sulphur mine with 2–3 hours to the summit. It is the "beginner" peak above 5,000 m.
- **Láscar (about 5,592 m, active):** about 11 hours from a 4,900 m base, plus an access fee. Access varies with volcanic activity.
- **Licancabur (5,916 m) and Sairecabur:** multi-day or expert-only expeditions; Licancabur is usually climbed from the Bolivian side.

Before any climb, spend at least two nights in San Pedro and do one 4,000 m tour first, and check that your travel insurance covers trekking above 5,000 m.

### ALMA observatory

ALMA normally offers free weekend visits to its Operations Support Facility at about 2,900 m, with a bus from San Pedro, but regular public visits are currently suspended. Chile's tourism board says work is under way to restart them, and ALMA ran special open days in October 2026. Under-5s are not admitted. The [stargazing guide](/stargazing-tours/) covers ALMA and Paranal.

### Bolivia: the Uyuni crossing

Most trips from San Pedro are 3-day one-way or 4-day return crossings via the Hito Cajón border (4,480 m). Day 1 takes in Laguna Blanca and Laguna Verde under Licancabur, the Dalí desert, Polques hot springs, the Sol de Mañana geysers and Laguna Colorada, reaching about 4,900 m. Our pick is the {{< gyg-link tour="774308" >}}4-Day Uyuni Salt Flats Tour (4.6/5 from 85 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}}. Treat it separately from the day tours; the [Uyuni guide](/uyuni-tours-from-san-pedro-de-atacama/) covers the border and lodging.

## Choose a lagoon or a thermal-pool visit

[Baltinache](/baltinache-tours/) and [Cejar](/laguna-cejar-tours/) are not the same experience right now. Baltinache has a bathing suspension, while Cejar's selected listing advertises floating. See the [direct comparison](/baltinache-vs-laguna-cejar/) before booking based on older reviews.

[Puritama](/puritama-hot-springs-tours/) is a different water experience again. The selected product provides transport and assistance but excludes pool admission. Choose it for warm soaking, not as a stand-in for saltwater floating.

## Compare the full cost

Reserve fees can change the apparent gap between two similar tours. One Moon Valley product includes entry, snacks and a cocktail; the simpler shared alternative leaves those out. The Piedras Rojas options have different meal wording even though their route names overlap.

Ask for the actual ticket amount for your nationality and age where relevant, how it must be paid and whether it needs to be purchased before departure. Avoid buying a second ticket for an option that already includes admission. Valle de la Luna, Cejar and the Socaire sites sell dated or timed tickets online, so at most of them you can no longer turn up with cash.

## Pickup and return can determine the better option

A central agency start is convenient if you stay nearby. Hotel pickup may be valuable from another address, but often has a defined urban boundary. Some products collect at accommodation and finish centrally, so compare both ends of the service.

The [where-to-stay guide](/where-to-stay/) and [no-car guide](/san-pedro-de-atacama-without-car/) help you match accommodation to the departures. An outlying lodge's own tours may use different pickup arrangements from an independently booked excursion.

## How to choose a tour operator

San Pedro has dozens of agencies on and around Caracoles street, many selling the same tours and often sharing each other's vans. These checks separate the good ones:

1. **SERNATUR registration.** Look the agency up on Chile's official tourism registry (serviciosturisticos.sernatur.cl), and avoid street sellers with no fixed office.
2. **Group size.** "Small group" should mean 8–12 people, not a 25-seat bus. Ask for the maximum.
3. **What is included.** Check entry tickets, breakfast or lunch, hotel pickup and drop-off. Some large operators drop you only in the centre.
4. **Altitude kit.** For El Tatio and the lagoons, ask whether they carry oxygen and a first-aid kit.
5. **Guide language.** Confirm a bilingual Spanish–English or Portuguese guide; do not assume one.
6. **Cancellation terms.** Many local agencies take a 50% deposit and treat no-shows as non-refundable. GetYourGuide and Viator usually offer free cancellation up to 24 hours before.

Names that come up repeatedly in traveller forums include Atacama Magic, praised for responsiveness; private-tour specialists such as Tastur, Vive Atacama, Sunway Atacama and Atacama Contact; Pukarumi, a large operator with its own fleet whose long tours some reviewers call gruelling; and Nomades, a reseller with clear price and fee breakdowns. Check current reviews before you choose one.

Outside peak season you can book ahead or on arrival; forum users report finding tours on the first evening without trouble. Book ahead for Christmas to February, Easter, Chilean holidays and anything with capped tickets, such as Piedras Rojas slots and Puritama.

## Renting a car instead

- **For:** it is cheaper for two or more people, and you can beat the convoys at Valle de la Luna and Chaxa.
- **Where to rent:** Calama airport; see [getting from Calama](/calama-airport-to-san-pedro-de-atacama/).
- **Fuel:** there is one Copec station in San Pedro and essentially none elsewhere, so fill up every night.
- **4x4:** not essential for the main sites, but helpful for fords and sand.
- **Avoid self-driving** to Salar de Tara (confusing) and to El Tatio in the dark if you are a nervous driver.
- **Hidden cost:** you still need every online ticket.

## Practicalities

- **Altitude sickness:** acclimatise for a day, drink water, eat light and skip alcohol the night before a high tour. Tell your guide straight away about persistent vomiting, confusion, chest tightness or a worsening headache. Pregnant travellers are commonly excluded from high-altitude tours and from Puritama; check with each operator.
- **Sun and temperature:** the UV is fierce and the air very dry. Expect about 25 °C by day and about 5 °C at dawn and dusk in town, and well below freezing at El Tatio.
- **Cash:** bring Chilean pesos for Tebinquiche, Rainbow Valley, Yerbas Buenas, Tulor, Baltinache and tips. ATMs in town work but charge heavy withdrawal fees, according to forum users.
- **Food on tours:** breakfast is standard on El Tatio, Rainbow Valley and lagoons tours, and lunch is sometimes included on the lagoons day. Bring snacks anyway.
- **Children:** Valle de la Luna has free entry for young children, Puritama and Cejar have child rates, and Laguna Piedra is not for under-6s. El Tatio does not admit under-9s, the featured Baltinache listing excludes under-6s, sandboarding usually starts at about 8, and ALMA does not admit under-5s.
- **Seasons:** December to March is the Altiplanic winter, with highland rain and storms; in January 2026 rain flooded parts of San Pedro. June to September brings snow: on 13 August 2026 the municipality said snow was still keeping El Tatio, Miscanti and Miñiques, Piedras Rojas, Rainbow Valley, Láscar and other high sites closed. Build a buffer day into any itinerary, and see the [best time to visit](/best-time-to-visit/).
- **Packing:** warm layers, a down jacket, hat and gloves for El Tatio; sunscreen (apply after swimming), sunglasses, a wide-brim hat and lip balm; a swimsuit, towel, sandals and 2 litres of fresh water to rinse off salt; a refillable bottle; downloaded or printed tickets; cash, a headlamp and any altitude medication you have discussed with a doctor. The [packing list](/what-to-pack/) has the rest.

## Make the schedule fit you

Count [full sightseeing days](/how-many-days/), protect meals and sleep, and read the [altitude guide](/altitude/) before committing to a highland tour. Start with one must-see experience, then add activities that change the scenery or pace instead of duplicating a route under another name. Check your exact dates against closures before you pay, because snow, wind and site works can change what is open.
