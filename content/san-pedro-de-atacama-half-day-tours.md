A half-day here usually means about four to six hours from pickup to drop-off, not two. Departure windows, pickup routes, admission arrangements and walking time vary from tour to tour. Some trips sold as a "morning tour" take seven or eight hours, so don't count on being back for lunch.

This guide compares the trips that leave you useful time for something else, which ones work on arrival day, and which combinations you can manage without racing the clock.

**If you have one free afternoon,** choose Moon Valley. **One free morning:** Rainbow Valley if you have five to six hours, or Puritama if you would like to relax. **For salt-lagoon scenery,** choose Cejar for bathing, when it is allowed, and Baltinache for its turquoise water, which you can only look at while the swimming suspension lasts. **On a clear evening,** add a stargazing session.

## 1. Valle de la Luna: best first half-day

You leave San Pedro's low adobe streets for a landscape that looks folded, dried and cracked. The Cordillera de la Sal rises in pale, rust-coloured ridges, and from the Duna Mayor route you look across an amphitheatre of layered rock toward Licancabur volcano. You get all of this without spending a whole day in a vehicle.

**Best for:** first-timers, arrival-day afternoons, landscape photography and anyone who wants a memorable outing without going up to the highest elevations.

**What a typical tour includes:** transport, a guide and stops at the Duna Mayor, Tres Marías and other authorised viewpoints, depending on the day's access. Expect a few short walks and no long trek. Most classic trips last about four hours, and sunset departures can run to about five.

**Ticket price:** the Reserva Nacional Los Flamencos access guide lists **CLP 10,800 for general Valle de la Luna admission** and CLP 5,400 for eligible students and seniors, as 2026 reference rates. Some tours include the ticket and others don't. Buy it only through the route your operator specifies, or from [Valle de la Luna's official ticket website](https://valledelaluna.com/) if you go independently.

**Two tours to compare:** the {{< gyg-link tour="466455" >}}Valle de la Luna Shared Sightseeing Tour (4.7/5 from 400+ reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} costs less, meets in the town centre and leaves the reserve ticket for you to buy. The {{< gyg-link tour="471706" >}}guided Valle de la Luna tour with entry, snacks and a cocktail (4.5/5 from 440+ reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} costs more but includes admission and hotel pickup. Choose the shared tour if you are staying centrally and don't mind buying the ticket yourself, and the guided one if you would like a single total price.

**Ckari viewpoint closure:** the official Valle de la Luna site says **Mirador de Ckari (Ckary) has been closed since 27 May 2026 until further notice**, after a safety review. Some older listings still promise sunset at Ckari, so ask where the sunset stop takes place before booking. The rest of the valley stays open.

**On arrival day:** it often works if your flight lands early, your transfer from Calama runs smoothly and you have time to check in. Avoid a fixed pickup that leaves little margin for delays, skip strenuous hiking on your first afternoon, and remember that San Pedro itself sits at about 2,400 m. Our [Calama airport transfer guide](/calama-airport-to-san-pedro-de-atacama/) covers the transfer times.

**A guided tour suits you if** you have no vehicle, would like someone else to handle timed entry, or want help reading the geology. **Independent entry suits you if** you have your own transport and are comfortable booking a timed ticket and following the site rules. You can't enter on foot at the park gate, and bicycles have their own restricted entry hours.

For an operator-by-operator comparison, see our [Valle de la Luna tour guide](/moon-valley-tours/).

## 2. Laguna Cejar: best for the salt-lagoon experience

Floating in very salty water is one of the classic Atacama memories, but not every lagoon on a tour itinerary is open for bathing. Compare what you are allowed to do, not only the turquoise photographs on a booking page.

Most afternoon circuits cross the Salar de Atacama to some combination of **Laguna Cejar, Laguna Piedra, Ojos del Salar and Laguna Tebinquiche**. The itinerary, access rules and names vary. In some periods the swimming stop is Laguna Piedra, not Cejar itself. Ask which lagoon is designated for bathing on your departure date, and whether there are showers or changing rooms.

**Best for:** travellers who want an unusual salt-flat experience, low-altitude sightseeing and a relaxed afternoon.

**Allow:** about 4–5.5 hours, depending on the route and the sunset stop. The tour may finish in town instead of at your accommodation. On GetYourGuide, the {{< gyg-link tour="614772" >}}Cejar Lagoon Tour with flotation and a cocktail{{< /gyg-link >}} has only four reviews so far, so the {{< viator-link path="San-Pedro-de-Atacama/San-Pedro-de-Atacama-Cejar-Lagoon/d5499-422580P12" >}}Laguna Cejar floating tour on Viator (5.0/5 from 80+ reviews){{< /viator-link >}} has the longer track record.

**Before paying, ask:**

1. Which lagoon can guests enter today?
2. Is the entrance fee included, or paid separately?
3. Are there showers, toilets and changing rooms at the site you visit?
4. Is pickup and return at your hotel, at an agency or in central San Pedro?
5. Is the itinerary shorter in winter, or when certain sectors are closed?

A salt lagoon is an ecosystem, not a swimming pool. Follow the posted rules on sunscreen, cosmetics and rinsing, stay on marked paths and don't collect salt. If floating is your main reason to book, check Baltinache's separate restrictions before choosing it as a substitute.

## 3. Puritama Hot Springs: best restorative half-day

The setting matters as much as the water: a narrow green strip of vegetation deep inside a dry canyon, with a chain of pools fed by warm springs. After two long excursions and a couple of pre-dawn pickups, this may be the afternoon you enjoy most.

**Best for:** a gentle day between demanding tours, couples, friends and anyone who would rather soak than sightsee from a vehicle.

The springs are about **28 km north of town** on route B-245 and sit well above San Pedro. The official Puritama website gives water temperatures of about **28–31°C**, warm but not hot. It sells a morning session from **09:30** and an afternoon session from **14:30**, each with its own exit time. Go by the time printed on your booking confirmation, because the site's pages have shown slightly different morning exit times.

**The most common mistake:** booking a cheap "Puritama tour" that is transport only, then finding the pool ticket must be bought separately. Each session has a capped number of places, so secure your pool admission before you book a transfer. On GetYourGuide the {{< gyg-link tour="832294" >}}Puritama Hot Springs thermal bath trip{{< /gyg-link >}} has a single review so far, which is too few to judge it on.

Bring swimwear, a towel, drinking water, sandals that grip on wet surfaces and layers for the drive back. Follow the posted rules on cosmetics in the pools. Some visitors find the paths, steps and altitude harder than the photographs suggest.

**Think twice before pairing Puritama with a strenuous high-altitude morning.** Even if the times allow it, fatigue and altitude symptoms matter more than a full schedule. If you have relevant health concerns, talk to a doctor before high-altitude travel.

Check admission and opening details at [Termas de Puritama](https://termasdepuritama.cl/), and see our [Puritama guide](/puritama-hot-springs-tours/).

## 4. Rainbow Valley and Yerbas Buenas: best for colour and culture

The rainbow here is in the rock. The hills show streaks of red, green, cream and ochre from their minerals, sometimes in sharp bands. Many tours combine those formations with the **Yerbas Buenas petroglyphs**, carvings that show how long Indigenous communities have lived in the region.

**Best for:** travellers who have already seen Moon Valley, families with older children who can walk in sun and wind, and anyone interested in landscape and archaeology.

**Allow:** five to six hours. Some operators leave around 07:00 and return around midday or early afternoon. You drive beyond the oasis and climb higher than at Moon Valley. The {{< viator-link path="San-Pedro-de-Atacama/Rainbow-Valley-Tour-from-San-Pedro-de-Atacama/d5499-6315CJCRAIN" >}}Rainbow Valley Tour on Viator (4.7/5 from 30+ reviews){{< /viator-link >}} follows this pattern, with the petroglyphs and a field breakfast.

**Check the fees:** confirm that the price covers both the Rainbow Valley and the Yerbas Buenas visits. Some local listings charge **separate entrance fees** for the archaeological and valley sectors, while others include them. Both stops appearing in the tour name doesn't mean both tickets are included.

Listen to the guide's explanation of the petroglyphs and respect the barriers around them. Don't touch or trace them, or photograph them from restricted areas. Give the rock art more than a quick photo stop.

This trip adds to Moon Valley; it doesn't replace it. You get more varied geology and more cultural background, but it takes longer and climbs higher.

## 5. Baltinache Hidden Lagoons: best for photography while bathing is restricted

The draw is obvious from the first view: startling blue and green water set into white salt crust, with bare mountains behind. The small lagoons are fragile, though, and the old selling point of effortless floating no longer applies.

**Swimming and floating at Baltinache have been suspended since 29 September 2026, with an observation-only period through January 2027 (as of November 2026).** No reopening date has been confirmed, and the rule may be reviewed. Ask your operator for the latest guidance from the community that manages the site before booking.

**Best for:** landscape photographs, salt-flat textures and visitors happy to admire the lagoons from the marked paths.

**Not ideal for:** anyone whose trip will feel incomplete without getting into the water. Book a Cejar or Piedra tour once you have checked that its bathing area is open, or choose Puritama if a warm soak is what you want.

A typical Baltinache trip, such as the {{< gyg-link tour="645617" >}}Baltinache Hidden Lagoons tour (4.5/5 from 150+ reviews){{< /gyg-link >}}, takes about four to five hours, and the state of the access road affects how long and how comfortable the drive is. Wear closed shoes that can cope with salt and uneven ground, bring water and stay on the permitted walkways. Your guide may add Vallecito or another nearby stop, so check the exact route; Baltinache tours differ.

## 6. Laguna Chaxa and Toconao: best for flamingos and a quieter morning

If the wildlife is your favourite part of a desert, Laguna Chaxa is one of the most rewarding shorter excursions. Flamingos feed in the shallow, briny water of the Salar de Atacama, with the Andes behind them. Some tours add the oasis village of Toconao and its traditional stone buildings.

**Best for:** birdwatchers, photographers, travellers who dislike high-altitude mornings and visitors who want a quieter outing than the long day tours.

**Check the length:** many excursions that list Chaxa are **long combined itineraries** that continue to Miscanti, Miñiques and Piedras Rojas, such as the {{< gyg-link tour="632997" >}}Red Stones, highland lagoons and Chaxa triple tour (4.7/5 from 340+ reviews){{< /gyg-link >}}, a full day. If you only have four to five hours, look for a **dedicated Chaxa and Toconao half-day** with a local agency. There is no well-reviewed half-day listing online, so ask in town; our [things to do guide](/things-to-do/) has more on the area.

The Los Flamencos reserve information lists Laguna Chaxa as a separately ticketed area managed through the Toconao community, with tickets sold at [comunidadtoconao.cl](https://comunidadtoconao.cl/). Check admission and opening hours when you travel. Flamingos are wild birds, so their numbers and distance from the paths change from day to day.

Take binoculars if you have them. The best visits leave time to watch the birds feeding instead of hurrying through ten photographs.

## 7. Valle de Marte (Valle de la Muerte): best short adventure close to town

Valle de Marte, also called Valle de la Muerte, sits closer to San Pedro than most of the well-known excursions. The dunes are spectacular, and several operators use the area for sandboarding or sunset outings.

**Best for:** a shorter adventure, a spare late afternoon, or travellers who have seen Moon Valley and want a different view of the salt range.

Know what you have booked. A **sandboarding lesson**, a **guided sightseeing trip** and an **independent viewpoint visit** are different products, with different equipment and effort. Sandboarding means climbing the dune again and again, so it is not a gentle activity. Wear sun protection, bring water and check that the activity includes safety equipment and instruction. These activities are sold mainly by agencies in town; see our [things to do guide](/things-to-do/).

If you want to combine Valle de Marte with Moon Valley, check the two separate tickets and how you will travel between them.

## 8. Pukará de Quitor: best do-it-yourself cultural outing

A good half-day doesn't always need a minibus. **Pukará de Quitor**, an archaeological site about **3 km from San Pedro**, fits into a short morning or late afternoon. It reminds you that the desert has long been lived in: communities farmed here and built defensive settlements long before modern tourism.

**Best for:** travellers with two or three free hours, people staying in town, and anyone interested in archaeology or a modest viewpoint walk.

You can usually reach the site by bicycle, on foot or with arranged transport, depending on conditions. Not every archaeological section and viewpoint is always open, so check the visitor circuit and admission fee with the site's administrator, and avoid hard effort in the midday heat.

A short cultural stop combines well with a slow lunch and an afternoon Moon Valley tour, as long as you have time to rest and drink water in between.

## 9. Stargazing: a short night experience, not an afternoon tour

An astronomy tour can feel like a second half-day, because many sessions run for two or three hours after dark. On a clear night the sky is striking even before the telescopes come out, and a good guide explains what you are looking at.

**Best for:** visitors with one free evening, photographers and anyone who wants something different after a day of sightseeing.

**Check before booking:** the Moon phase and where the Moon sits during the session; whether the tour runs around full Moon; the cloud cancellation and rebooking policy; the language; how long you spend at the telescopes; whether the return to your hotel is included; and the clothing advice. Standing still at night gets cold, even after a hot afternoon.

**Two well-reviewed sessions:** the {{< gyg-link tour="542449" >}}San Pedro de Atacama: Stargazing Experience (4.7/5 from 800+ reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} includes telescope viewing and a souvenir night photograph, and the {{< gyg-link tour="209091" >}}Stargazing and Andean Astronomy with Wine session (4.7/5 from 800+ reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} adds Andean sky lore and a glass of wine.

**Best pairing:** Moon Valley in the afternoon, dinner, then stargazing, if the times leave you a real break. Avoid an astronomy session right before an El Tatio tour with a pre-dawn pickup unless you are happy to lose sleep.

## Best morning tours vs best afternoon tours

### Best morning choices

**Rainbow Valley and Yerbas Buenas** make a strong first pick if you have the whole morning and part of lunchtime. Departures are often early, when the air is cooler. **Puritama** works once you have the morning pool session and a matching transfer. **Laguna Chaxa** is good for wildlife if you can find a dedicated short outing instead of a full-day Altiplanic Lagoons circuit. A self-guided visit to Pukará de Quitor is simpler still.

### Best afternoon choices

**Valle de la Luna** is the classic afternoon excursion, with the light shifting on the dunes and salt formations. **Laguna Cejar or Piedra** and **Baltinache** also run in the afternoon; check what the ticket lets you do. **Puritama** has an afternoon session, but you need to match the drive to the pool exit time. **Valle de Marte** can suit a short sunset outing.

### Best evening choice

A **guided stargazing tour** after dinner, on a night with a suitable Moon and a workable cloud forecast.

## Can you combine two half-day tours in one day?

You can, but two half-days often add up to a full and tiring day once you count hotel pickup, waiting, lunch, travel and the evening return. These pairings tend to work, or not:

| Combination | Our verdict | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| **Easy morning in town + Moon Valley afternoon** | **Best arrival or first-day plan** | Don't count on a late flight; allow time for lunch and rest |
| **Rainbow Valley morning + Moon Valley afternoon** | **Doable if the times line up** | The Rainbow tour's real return, lunch, same-day ticket timing |
| **Puritama morning + Moon Valley afternoon** | **Possible but busy** | Pool exit time, transfer, changing and lunch |

**Don't book two tours on their advertised departure and return times alone.** Ask both operators whether the first tour can run late and where the second one meets. A "4-hour tour" may finish somewhere other than where it picked you up.

## Sample half-day itineraries

### If you have only one afternoon

- **Early afternoon:** check in, eat something light and refill your water bottles.
- **Mid to late afternoon:** a guided Moon Valley tour with a valid timed entrance ticket.
- **Evening:** dinner in San Pedro. Add stargazing only if you have a comfortable gap, the Moon is suitable and the session is running.

### If you have one full day but want two shorter outings

- **Morning:** Rainbow Valley and Yerbas Buenas, or a booked Puritama session.
- **Midday:** return, have lunch and rest.
- **Afternoon:** Moon Valley. If your morning trip runs late, switch to something without a fixed meeting time instead of risking a missed pickup.

### If this is your first full day at altitude

- **Morning:** a slow breakfast, a walk around San Pedro and perhaps Pukará de Quitor if you feel well.
- **Afternoon:** Moon Valley or a lower salt-flat outing.
- **Following days:** El Tatio or Piedras Rojas once you have had time to acclimatise, depending on your health and comfort.

### If highland rain cancels a planned tour

Don't assume an alternative will be open. Check access first, then ask your operator about lower trips such as Moon Valley, Cejar, Chaxa or something in town. Rain, flooding and road damage can close routes well away from the storm itself. Our [best time to visit guide](/best-time-to-visit/) covers the summer rain months.

## How much do half-day tours cost?

The total can have up to three parts: the operator's tour price, the attraction's entrance ticket, and anything the listing leaves out (meals, a towel or the ride back to your hotel). Starting prices help you browse, but they are a poor way to compare tours with different inclusions.

Moon Valley shows why. In November 2026 the reserve lists **CLP 10,800 for adult general admission**. A cheap shared tour that leaves this out can cost more in total than a pricier listing with entry included. The same goes for Puritama, where the pool booking and the transfer are often sold separately.

Before you pay, compare these six points:

1. **The real departure and return times**, including the pickup window.
2. **Where you start and finish**, especially if your hotel is outside central San Pedro.
3. **Entrance fees**: included, prepaid separately or paid at the site.
4. **What you actually do**: bathing or only looking, sandboarding or sightseeing, telescope time or a photo stop.
5. **Physical demands**: the highest altitude, the walking and any minimum age.
6. **Cancellation rules**, including what happens if the site closes, the sky clouds over or the trip stops partway.

## What to pack for a short desert excursion

Even four hours in the Atacama can take you through several temperature bands. The safe default is **water, sun protection, a brimmed hat, sunglasses, closed walking shoes and a light layer**. Bring a charged phone, an offline copy of your ticket and enough Chilean pesos for any fees paid on site.

Then add for your tour:

- **Moon Valley and Rainbow Valley:** extra water, sturdy shoes and a windproof layer; sandals let grit in on the trails.
- **Cejar and Puritama:** swimwear if bathing is allowed, a towel, dry clothes and footwear for wet ground.
- **Baltinache:** shoes for uneven salt paths; no swimming unless the local rules change.
- **Chaxa:** binoculars, a camera zoom and patience; keep to the marked routes.
- **Stargazing:** a warm jacket, long trousers, closed shoes and perhaps gloves; evenings can be surprisingly cold.
- **Sandboarding:** clothes and footwear suited to the activity, plus the provider's safety equipment.

## Altitude and safety: the fact that matters most

San Pedro de Atacama sits at **about 2,400 m above sea level**. Moon Valley and the salt-flat tours stay close to that height. Puritama and Rainbow Valley climb higher, and El Tatio reaches about **4,300 m**. Altitude can affect you more than the tour's advertised length suggests.

Take your first day gently if you have come from sea level, drink plenty of water and pay attention to headache, nausea, unusual tiredness, dizziness or breathlessness. Serious symptoms call for an immediate descent and a medical check. A short tour with little walking still takes you to the same altitude. See our [altitude-by-tour guide](/altitude/), and get personal medical advice before you travel if you have a relevant condition.

The other safety basics matter just as much: don't take unmarked shortcuts across the salt flats, don't enter closed lagoons, keep back from unstable cliff edges and respect the local community rules. In summer, sudden storms in the highlands can close roads even when it is sunny in town.

## Our verdict

For most visitors, **book one afternoon at Moon Valley**, one different kind of outing (**Rainbow Valley, a salt-lagoon trip or Puritama**) and, if the sky and the Moon allow, an astronomy session. That mix shows you more than three versions of the same salt-flat trip.

If your time is short, don't chase every attraction. Pick the experience you want most, whether that is **landscape, wildlife, water, geology or stars**, and check that its real schedule fits your arrival, your energy and your budget. Leave a little slack in the day, because tours here often run later than advertised.
