## The current difference is access to the water

As checked on 9 October 2026, the featured Baltinache listing says swimming and floating have been suspended since 29 September. It describes a sightseeing visit with an added Magic Bus stop and an expected December reopening without a confirmed date. The Cejar listing still advertises a floating stop.

That makes the answer more specific than “both are beautiful.” If floating is the purpose of your booking, ask the Cejar provider to confirm current bathing access. If photographs and desert scenery are enough, compare the routes and departure times of both.

| Decision | Baltinache | Cejar circuit |
|---|---|---|
| Current featured format | Lagoon sightseeing plus Magic Bus stop | Floating advertised, plus lagoon sightseeing |
| Water access | Temporarily suspended in current notice | Reconfirm for your visit date |
| Advertised duration | About 4.5 hours | About 5.5 hours |
| Refreshments | AM breakfast or PM cocktail | Cocktail |

## Choose Baltinache for scenery during the suspension

The seven turquoise lagoons set against salt formations are the main visual attraction. The current operator describes time on paths and viewpoints and a modified route, rather than replacing the swimming stop with permission elsewhere.

Do not book solely because an older review describes a swim. Reviews remain evidence of that person’s past experience, not current access. We explain the notice and conflicting old itinerary icons in the [Baltinache tour guide](/baltinache-tours/).

## Choose Cejar for the floating-focused circuit

The selected product pairs the Cejar visit with Ojos del Salar and Tebinquinche. The appeal is the combination of bathing where authorised and salt-flat scenery. Ask which pool is used for floating; a tour’s headline name can refer to a wider lagoon complex.

The [Cejar guide](/laguna-cejar-tours/) also makes the separate admission fees and central return clear. A five-and-a-half-hour listing is not all time in the water, and not every stop is a swimming area.

## Compare the complete cost and timing

Both featured products charge entry separately. Baltinache’s temporary notice makes the fee especially date-sensitive, while Cejar’s supplier asks for the reserve payments before the excursion. Ask for the total applicable to your party and payment method.

Pickup is listed for both, but the published itineraries finish centrally at Caracoles 600. Confirm whether that fits your accommodation. If you intend to [stargaze](/stargazing-tours/) afterwards, leave enough time to return, change and eat before the next meeting point.

## Do you need both?

Usually not on a short first visit. With [three full days](/3-day-itinerary/), one lagoon outing leaves more variety for [Moon Valley](/moon-valley-tours/) and a highland day. Book both if lagoon landscapes are a particular interest and you have time, rather than because a checklist lists two different names.

If you want warm soaking instead of saltwater floating, [Puritama](/puritama-hot-springs-tours/) is the more relevant alternative. Its transport product excludes thermal-pool entry, so the comparison again needs the full visit cost.

## What could change the recommendation?

A confirmed Baltinache reopening would bring the floating comparison back into play. A closure at Cejar would change it in the opposite direction. We keep the notice date visible because this recommendation rests on current access, not a permanent claim that one lagoon is better.

Before reserving, ask one direct question: “Is bathing permitted on my date, and which part of the itinerary changes if it is not?” The answer should come from your selected operator and current local rules.
