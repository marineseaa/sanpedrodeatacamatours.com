## What makes the Cejar circuit different?

This is a half-day lagoon outing, rather than the long volcanic-plateau circuit sold as [Piedras Rojas and the Altiplanic Lagoons](/piedras-rojas-tours/). The itinerary pairs a floating stop with contrasting salt-flat scenery and a closing cocktail. Choose it if you want time around the water without allocating a whole day to the highlands.

The selected listing has only a small number of product-specific reviews. We include it because it matches the actual Cejar itinerary and clearly separates the entry fee, not because a high rating from a small sample proves it is the best operator in town.

## Cejar, Piedra, Ojos and Tebinquinche

Tours often use “Laguna Cejar” as the headline for the wider lagoon visit. Ask the operator which pool is currently authorised for floating; do not assume every named body of water is a swimming area. **Ojos del Salar and Tebinquinche are additional itinerary stops**, not a promise that you may swim wherever the van stops.

The current product describes approximately five and a half hours, pickup in San Pedro and a finish at Caracoles 600. The route can change, so use it as a guide to the experience rather than a precise minute-by-minute timetable.

## Compare Cejar with Baltinache before buying both

The decisive current difference is bathing access. The featured [Baltinache listing](/baltinache-tours/) reports suspended swimming from 29 September 2026, while the Cejar listing still advertises floating. For someone specifically looking for buoyancy in saltwater, that makes Cejar the product to investigate first.

If you mainly want photographs of turquoise pools and salt formations, Baltinache remains relevant as a sightseeing outing. With only [three days](/3-day-itinerary/), one lagoon excursion is generally enough variety alongside Moon Valley and a highland day. Our [direct comparison](/baltinache-vs-laguna-cejar/) explains what you would give up by picking only one.

## Know what is included

The tour lists hotel pickup, a bilingual guide and a cocktail. Park admission is extra, paid before departure, preferably in cash. Ask for the fee breakdown for every stop rather than budgeting only for the headline lagoon. The operator also describes a surcharge for payment links.

The current booking information asks for reservations at least 24 hours before departure. If you are booking at the last minute, obtain confirmation rather than assuming an available calendar slot has settled the entry-ticket arrangements.

## Prepare for the water and the desert

Bring swimwear, a towel, drinking water, sun protection and a layer for later. Check the current rules for sunscreen and bathing before entering: protected lagoons can limit products and time in the water. Follow the local instructions about rinsing off and staying within the authorised area.

Floating is not the same as having an accessible pool with easy entry. Ask about the approach, steps, surfaces and changing facilities if those affect your group. The selected product excludes children under 6 and pregnant travellers.

## Leave enough room afterwards

Keep the return flexible, particularly if you plan an astronomy tour later the same evening. You will want time to rinse, change and eat. If that would produce a rushed connection, reserve [stargazing](/stargazing-tours/) for a different night and use the lagoon afternoon as the slower part of your stay.
