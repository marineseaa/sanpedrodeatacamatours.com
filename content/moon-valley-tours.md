## Closures in 2026 {#closures-in-2026}

**Mirador de Ckari.** The official site says the Ckari viewpoint, the clifftop where sunset tours traditionally ended, has been closed until further notice since 27 May 2026 for a safety assessment after an earthquake. No reopening date has been published. Some tour listings still name Ckari, so ask the operator where the tour watches sunset before you pay.

**Staff-training days.** The official notice lists full closure days with no public entry in October 2026; the last is on 27 October. More training days may follow in later months, so check the notice on valledelaluna.com before you book a date.

**Weather.** Sectors can close for strong wind and dust in winter and for the rare summer rain of January to March.

### Where to watch sunset now

1. **On a sunset tour.** Operators have adapted, and guides now finish at an authorised spot in the Cordillera de la Sal. Several end with a cocktail and snacks.
2. **At Valle de Marte (Valle de la Muerte).** This separate valley lies about 2 km from town, with its own ticket, and its viewpoints look across the dunes to Licancabur.
3. **Both in one day.** Visit Valle de la Luna by car in the morning or early afternoon, then drive to Valle de Marte for sunset.

The best light is often behind you: as the sun drops in the west, look east, where the Andes and Licancabur turn pink.

## Book the right reserve ticket

The shared listing tells guests to buy admission before departure through the reserve’s booking website. Follow the link in the supplier’s confirmation and match the visit date. Ask for help before the meeting time if the ticket categories or payment process are unclear.

The more inclusive alternative lists the Valle de la Luna ticket in the reservation. Reconfirm this for the selected option, then avoid buying a duplicate ticket. A tour title containing “Valle de la Luna” alone does not tell you whether admission is bundled.

### Buying the ticket yourself

1. Go to **valledelaluna.com**, the site of the Asociación Indígena Valle de la Luna. Look-alike guide sites do not sell official tickets.
2. Choose **Buy tickets**, then your date and the visitor categories.
3. Pay by card online.
4. Save the QR code offline, because the signal inside the valley is patchy.
5. Arrive within the entry window for your vehicle or bike, and show the QR code and any discount ID at reception.
6. Remember that the ticket is valid only for the date on your order. It cannot be moved to another day.

## Valle de Marte as the sunset alternative

Valle de Marte, also called Valle de la Muerte, is a separate valley in the same salt range, about 2 km from town, with its own manager and its own ticket. It is known for its tall dunes and sandboarding, and its upper viewpoints look across to Licancabur at sunset. It works as a DIY sunset after a morning in Valle de la Luna, or as a short outing in its own right. The [tour comparison](/tours/) covers sandboarding there.

## How the valley formed and who runs it

Valle de la Luna is a basin of eroded salt, gypsum and clay ridges in the Cordillera de la Sal, the salt range between San Pedro and the Salar de Atacama. Ancient salt lakes evaporated again and again, leaving thick layers of salt, gypsum and clay. Pressure from the rising Andes then folded and lifted those layers into ridges, and wind and the occasional rain carved them into dunes, cliffs, canyons and free-standing figures. The association that runs the site says these rocks began forming about 33 million years ago. The surface glitters with salt crystals, and on cool evenings the crust crackles as it contracts.

The name comes from the grey-white crust, the craters and the total absence of plants. The valley has been a Santuario de la Naturaleza since 1982 and forms part of the Reserva Nacional Los Flamencos.

Since the early 2000s, the Asociación Indígena Valle de la Luna has co-managed the site with CONAF, Chile’s forest and parks service. The association brings together six Lickanantay (Atacameño) communities: Coyo, Larache, San Pedro, Sequitor, Solor and Quitor. Your ticket money goes to the communities and the reserve.

## Arrival-day planning

Moon Valley can suit the beginning of your visit because it avoids the major highland ascent of [El Tatio](/el-tatio-geysers-tours/). That does not mean you should book a tight flight-to-tour connection. Allow for baggage collection, the [Calama transfer](/calama-airport-to-san-pedro-de-atacama/), checking in and getting to the meeting point.

If arrival timing is uncertain, use the first afternoon for settling in and put the tour on the following day. Heat, exposure and walking still matter, especially if you have just travelled overnight. ## What to bring and what to leave out

- Your ticket QR code saved offline, plus ID for any discount
- At least 2 litres of water per person
- Sunscreen, lip balm, sunglasses and a wide-brimmed hat
- Comfortable closed shoes with grip
- A windproof jacket and a warm layer for after sunset or an early start
- Snacks, because the included snack on one option does not replace planning dinner
- Cash if your operator collects the ticket money at its office

Leave the drone behind: the official rules prohibit drone flights. Keep to the marked paths to protect the fragile formations, and stay behind the barriers. One of the Tres Marías figures is reported to have been broken by a visitor who climbed it for a photo.

### Safety and accessibility

There is no shade, and the sun is strong at this altitude, so cover up even on a cool day. The temperature drops fast after sunset. Wind carries grit along the ridges, and sunglasses and a buff help. With children, watch the drop around Mina Victoria and the soft sand on Duna Mayor.

The two products have different age and mobility restrictions. The cheaper shared listing excludes under-8s and over-70s as well as pregnant travellers and people with mobility impairments. The alternative has different listed limits, but an absent restriction does not prove an accessible route. The terrain is sandy and uneven; ask about the actual walking before booking, and contact the association if you need help on site.

## Is Valle de la Luna worth it?

Yes, even with Ckari closed: the view from Duna Mayor is worth the ticket on its own. Take a tour if you have no car, travel solo or want a guided sunset without the logistics. Go on your own by car or bike if you prefer quiet trails and cooler air in the morning, then catch sunset at Valle de Marte. Check the official closure notice for your date before you book, because training days and weather closures can change at short notice.
