## The town and the excursions are different exposures

Staying in San Pedro does not put every tour at the same elevation. The featured Piedras Rojas listings describe stops around 4,200 metres; the El Tatio listing places the geyser field above 4,300 metres. The selected Uyuni expedition approaches 5,000 metres and includes overnight stays in the Bolivian highlands. The season changes these high days too, so check the [best time to visit](/best-time-to-visit/) before you fix the order.

A daytime ascent followed by a return to lower sleeping elevation differs from sleeping high. The [CDC Yellow Book](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/high-altitude-travel-and-altitude-illness.html) explains why sleeping altitude and the rate of ascent matter. Do not apply a sleeping-altitude guideline as if it were a guarantee for a rapid daytime excursion.

## Build the itinerary with room to adjust

Start with easier activities around the lower desert and put the highest excursions later where possible. Avoid heavy exertion and alcohol during your initial adjustment. Sleep, eat normally and carry water; forcing excessive water is not a proven way to prevent altitude illness.

The [three-day itinerary](/3-day-itinerary/) gives you a choice of one major highland trip, while the [four-day plan](/4-day-itinerary/) separates two. Neither table is medical clearance. If you arrive from another high-altitude destination, your circumstances differ from those of someone flying directly from the coast.

## Recognise symptoms early

A new headache after ascent, particularly with nausea, dizziness, fatigue or reduced appetite, may indicate acute mountain sickness. Tell your guide and **do not go higher while symptoms continue**. Seek medical assessment rather than dismissing symptoms as a lack of fitness.

Breathlessness at rest, confusion or trouble walking steadily are urgent warning signs. Seek immediate medical help and arrange descent as safely as possible; do not wait to see whether tomorrow’s itinerary feels easier. Remote excursions can be far from care, so know how the operator communicates and responds.

## Fitness does not make you immune

Strong hiking fitness is not a reliable shield against altitude illness. A short walk at a high stop may feel unexpectedly difficult even if you normally exercise. Previous successful travel to altitude is useful information, but it does not guarantee the same response on a faster or higher itinerary.

Talk with a clinician familiar with altitude travel before departure if you have relevant heart, lung or other medical conditions, have previously had serious altitude problems, are pregnant, or are considering preventive medication. Medication decisions and dosing belong in that consultation, not in a tour booking form.

## Product restrictions still apply

The featured [El Tatio tour](/el-tatio-geysers-tours/) currently excludes under-9s and over-60s along with specified conditions. The [Piedras Rojas options](/piedras-rojas-tours/) use different age limits. These are conditions of the selected products and should be checked for your exact reservation.

Do not look for a less explicit listing merely to bypass a restriction. Ask the operator about the actual route, highest point, time spent high, walking and arrangements for someone who needs to turn back. “Oxygen included” is not a guarantee that a rapid ascent is appropriate.

## What to ask before booking

Ask where you sleep, the maximum route altitude, how quickly the vehicle climbs, whether communication works along the route and what happens if a passenger becomes unwell. For [Uyuni](/uyuni-tours-from-san-pedro-de-atacama/), add evacuation arrangements and the remoteness of each overnight stop.

If a high excursion is unsuitable, consider the [Moon Valley options](/moon-valley-tours/) or a less intensive stay around town, with professional advice where needed. There is no requirement to visit every high-altitude sight to enjoy the Atacama.
