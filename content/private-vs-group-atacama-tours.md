San Pedro de Atacama is a small desert village, but the places you visit from it spread across a wide range of altitudes. You can spend an afternoon walking among the salt-and-clay ridges of Valle de la Luna, leave before dawn for the steaming vents of El Tatio at about 4,300 metres, or spend a full day climbing into the Andes to blue lagoons below volcanoes. Most visitors book a guided trip for each of these. The choice comes down to how much independence you want to pay for.

Before you pay a premium for things you will not use, or book the cheapest seat and find that entrance fees, a long round of hotel pickups and a full vehicle were not what you expected, compare the two on the points below.

**In short:** choose shared for a first visit on a budget, for the short trips close to town and if you like meeting other travellers. Consider private when you are four or more, travel with children or someone with limited mobility, have a serious photography interest, or want a particular stop or pace. Before paying, get an all-in written quote for your exact date and party size.

## Private vs shared Atacama tours compared

| Question | Shared or group tour | Private tour |
|---|---|---|
| Who is in the vehicle? | Other paying travellers | Your party, usually with a guide and driver of your own |
| How is it priced? | Usually per person | Often per vehicle or party, sometimes per person |
| Best for | Solo travellers, couples and anyone watching the budget | Families, groups, photographers and travellers who need their own pace |
| Pickup | A set meeting point or hotel pickup in central San Pedro | Usually more adaptable, but confirm the exact address |
| Departure and return | Fixed, or within a narrow window | Can move within park hours and the operator's limits |
| Time at stops | Shared between the group's interests | Can favour the stops you most want to see |
| Entrance fees | Often extra | Also often extra: never assume they are included |
| Meals | Depends on the listing | Depends on the quote |
| Language | May be bilingual or multilingual | You can ask for a guide in your language; get it confirmed |
| Weather changes | The operator sets the revised plan | Easier to discuss alternatives, but closures still apply |
| Main drawback | Less personal time and flexibility | A higher total cost for small parties |

A small group is not a private tour. A tour capped at eight or twelve passengers still keeps to a shared schedule. "Private transport" does not always mean a private guide either, so check both.

## Other Atacama trips to book on a shared seat

Several of the trips around San Pedro work well in a group whichever way you take the big highland days. Sunset at **Valle de la Luna**, a float in **Laguna Cejar**, a soak at **Puritama Hot Springs** and an evening of **stargazing** are short, close to town and lower than El Tatio or the **Altiplanic Lagoons**.

### Six costs to check before you compare

1. **Guide and driver:** is the driver also the guide? Is a guide who speaks English confirmed?
2. **Park admission:** is it included, prepaid or bought separately? Which age or residency rate applies?
3. **Vehicle:** is it yours alone? How many seats and how much luggage space does it have?
4. **Food and water:** is breakfast served on a pre-dawn trip? Is lunch provided on the ten-hour day?
5. **Pickup:** is pickup from your accommodation included? Does it reach places outside central San Pedro?
6. **Cancellation and changes:** are the terms for a private booking the same as for a shared seat? What happens if the park closes or you cannot travel?

If a price looks unusually low, ask what it leaves out. A low headline can end up costing more once park tickets, meals and local transport are added.

## Which Atacama tours are worth booking privately?

### Valle de la Luna: a shared tour is usually enough

**Usual choice: a shared tour.** Valle de la Luna makes a good first afternoon: it is close to town, and most tours take about four hours. The salt formations, the dune viewpoints and the colours of the Cordillera de la Sal at sunset look the same from a private vehicle. Our [half-day tours guide](/san-pedro-de-atacama-half-day-tours/) compares it with the other short trips from town.

A private guide helps if you want to time stops for photography, hear the geology explained at length or set your own walking pace. Park opening hours, trails and access rules still apply to your visit, and a private booking cannot open a closed viewpoint.

**Ask:** which stops are open today? Is admission included? Where does the tour finish? Is the sunset spot in the quote still open?

See our [Valle de la Luna tours](/moon-valley-tours/) guide for the shared options.

### El Tatio geysers: a private tour buys comfort and attention, not a later start

**Usual choice: shared for value, private for photographers or a party that wants its own guide.** El Tatio is a pre-dawn trip to a geothermal field at about 4,300 metres. A shared tour such as the {{< gyg-link tour="345946" >}}Tatio Geysers at Sunrise tour (from about US$72 per person, 4.7/5 from 1,150+ reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} gets everyone to the field for the early-morning steam, with breakfast on the checked listing.

A private vehicle means no round of hotel pickups, more time for photography and breaks when your group wants them. The {{< viator-link path="San-Pedro-de-Atacama/Guided-Visit-to-Tatio-Geyser-with-Geyser-Blanco-Hot-Springs/d5499-212507P7" >}}Private Tour of Geyser del Tatio on Viator (4.7/5 from 85+ reviews){{< /viator-link >}} includes breakfast, park entry and a stop at the Geyser Blanco hot springs, and is priced per traveller, so enter your party size to see the total. Booking privately does not let you leave hours later and still see the same thick steam: the cold air at first light makes it visible, so expect the same early start as the shared tours.

The park's 2026 entry rules set some limits that affect both kinds of trip. Tour groups need one guide for every 12 passengers, with an assistant guide added from the 13th passenger. A driver may also act as the guide only on a private tour in a vehicle for up to seven passengers. Children under 8 are not admitted, without exception ([El Tatio entry regulations 2026](https://eltatio.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Reglamento-de-Ingreso-y-Permanencia-2026.pdf)).

**Ask:** the actual departure time, the expected temperature, how long you walk and stand, what equipment and first-aid supplies the guide carries, the entry fee, breakfast, and whether the guide can change the plan if someone feels unwell.

A private tour does not make high altitude safe for children or for anyone sensitive to it. Read our [altitude advice](/altitude/) and the current El Tatio rules before you book.

### Piedras Rojas and the Altiplanic Lagoons: the strongest case for going private

**Usual choice: shared if cost matters, private if your group can afford the flexibility.** This is a long, high-altitude day of red rock, turquoise water, open plains and volcanoes, often with stops at Socaire, Toconao or the Chaxa lagoon, depending on the itinerary. You spend much of the day in the vehicle, so your own group can be more comfortable than a mixed party with different ideas about stops and photo time.

A private booking does not make the day spontaneous. The Socaire community sells tickets for the Miscanti and Miñiques lagoons only through its website, and at check-in its staff ask for the ticket's QR code and the vehicle's registration plate ([Socaire community](https://socairechile.cl/)). A private guide works to that booking too, so confirm that the operator is buying the right combination of entries.

**Ask:** which lagoons are included, and for how long at each? Does the vehicle stop for photos where it is safe? Are there toilets on the way? Are breakfast and lunch included? Who books the Socaire tickets? What happens if the high road closes?

Our [Piedras Rojas tours](/piedras-rojas-tours/) guide compares the shared full-day options, and [El Tatio vs Piedras Rojas](/el-tatio-vs-piedras-rojas/) helps if you have time for only one.

### Laguna Cejar and the salt lagoons: usually shared

This is a shorter, simpler trip, and a shared tour gives you what you need: transport, the visit and practical advice on floating in the salt water where it is allowed. The {{< gyg-link tour="614772" >}}Cejar Lagoon Tour - Flotation, Cocktail & More! (4.5/5, only 4 reviews so far){{< /gyg-link >}} is one option; with so few reviews, read the inclusions closely. A private trip helps if you want a gentle pace or need help with access and changing. Check the current swimming rules before you go, because they differ between lagoons and can change. Our [Laguna Cejar tours](/laguna-cejar-tours/) guide has the details.

### Puritama Hot Springs: secure the pool booking first

A private transfer can be pleasant for a family, but you need the timed bathing admission and workable transport times first. Ask whether the product includes admission and the right bathing session. The {{< gyg-link tour="832294" >}}Puritama Hot Springs: Thermal Bath from San Pedro de Atacama (5.0/5, only 1 review so far){{< /gyg-link >}} has very little feedback yet. A transfer you arrange yourself plus a pool reservation made in advance can work out well if the times line up; see our [Puritama Hot Springs tours](/puritama-hot-springs-tours/) guide.

For astronomy, the number of people sharing a telescope and the guide's attention can matter more than who drives you to the site. ## Who should choose a private tour?

### Families with children

A vehicle of your own makes snack stops, luggage, car seats and a tired child easier to manage. It does not change minimum ages or altitude precautions. Ask the operator in writing about child seats and the age rules for each place; El Tatio admits no children under 8. For younger children, a lower trip such as Valle de la Luna is an easier start than a long day above 4,000 metres.

### Groups of four to six

This is the group most likely to find a private booking competitive. Ask for a fixed group quote, then divide the all-in amount by the number of people. Check whether five or six people need a bigger vehicle, which may change the rate. The private tours checked for this guide on Viator take up to four people, so a price for "up to four" does not cover six.

### Couples and honeymooners

A shared tour often leaves more of the budget for a better hotel or another trip. Private is a deliberate splurge: fewer distractions, a guide who talks only to you, and perhaps a quieter time at a viewpoint where the rules allow it. The scenery is the same either way, so pay for privacy only if you will value it.

### Photographers

The extra cost is easier to justify when you care about time at a particular viewpoint, the direction of the light, carrying equipment and not rushing between stops. Ask whether the guide knows photography well; not every private tour is a photo workshop. Weather, reserve access and the permitted stopping places still limit what you can do.

### Solo travellers

Shared is usually the obvious choice on cost. You may meet other travellers, and you do not pay for empty seats. A private tour makes sense only for a specialised interest, an accessibility need, or when you can find travel companions to split the cost.

### Travellers with limited mobility or health concerns

With private transport you can discuss walking distances, rest stops, pace and how you get in and out of the vehicle in advance. It does not guarantee accessible trails or prevent altitude illness. Ask about the terrain, steps, toilets and vehicle boarding, and check with a doctor if altitude is a concern for your health. Do not book on the word "easy" in a listing alone.

## Are small-group tours a good middle ground?

Often, yes. A small group can give you some of the room of a private tour at a per-person price. "Small group" is not a fixed number, though: listings cap groups at six, eight, twelve or more. Ask for the maximum number of paying passengers, not the average. At El Tatio, the park's rule of one guide per 12 passengers sets how many guides a group needs. It does not cap your booking at twelve.

A useful small-group listing tells you:

- The maximum number of passengers and the type of vehicle.
- Whether the guide speaks your language for the whole trip.
- Where pickup and drop-off happen, and how many collection stops to expect.
- Whether your group can be merged with another group or operator.
- Exactly which admissions, food and cancellation terms apply.

When two shared tours are close in price, choose the one that states its group-size cap and inclusions clearly over one that promises vague "premium" service.

## How much flexibility does a private tour give you?

**Usually:** more say over the length of breaks, photo stops where they are allowed, the pace, meal preferences and the pickup point. **Not necessarily:** any departure time you like, off-road driving, changes to park entry times, a guaranteed sunset spot, access to closed trails or a way round a road closed for safety.

This matters most during the *invierno altiplánico*, the summer highland rainy season from late December to March. Rain and storms can close the roads to the high-altitude sights. A private operator can sometimes suggest another trip that is still open, but cannot override a closure.

For a January or February visit, choose an operator with a clear bad-weather policy and leave a spare day. ### Questions to send an operator

> Hello, we are [number] people visiting San Pedro de Atacama on [date]. Is this tour shared or private for our group only? What is the maximum number of passengers? Please confirm the total price including entrance fees, the exact route and stops, the pickup location, the guide's language, meals, and what happens if a road or site closes because of the weather. Thank you.

For a photography tour, add: "Which stops can we spend longer at, and can we move the departure to match the light within the park's opening hours?"

For a family tour, add the children's ages and ask about child seats and minimum-age rules.

## Should you book through GetYourGuide, Viator or directly?

All three can work. Booking platforms let you compare reviews and published cancellation terms in one place; local operators may give you more detail on a custom itinerary. Neither route always means a lower price, better service or more protection.

On a marketplace, read the individual product's inclusions and cancellation deadline, not only the platform's general banner. Booking direct, check the operator's business details, contact methods, refund procedure and how payment is taken. Chile's national tourism service, SERNATUR, runs an official search of registered tourism providers, and the El Tatio rules require guides there to hold a current SERNATUR registration. Registration is useful to know but does not guarantee quality.

## Two ways to spend three days

### Plan A: save money with shared tours

**Day 1:** fly into Calama, settle in and take a shared Valle de la Luna afternoon tour if your arrival time and energy allow.

**Day 2:** a shared Piedras Rojas and Altiplanic Lagoons day, if you have acclimatised and conditions allow. It is a long day, so keep the evening free.

**Day 3:** a shared El Tatio sunrise tour, if your health and acclimatisation allow, then rest in the afternoon. If you want to go stargazing, book it for an earlier evening so you can move it if the sky is cloudy.

If you are arriving from sea level, do not go straight up into the high Andes. An extra day at San Pedro's own altitude is often more comfortable; ask a doctor about any personal altitude concerns.

### Plan B: pay for one private day

**Day 1:** a relaxed arrival and a shared Valle de la Luna tour.

**Day 2:** a private Piedras Rojas and Altiplanic Lagoons day, with the route, the Socaire tickets and the photo stops agreed in advance.

**Day 3:** a shared El Tatio tour, or a lower trip if the high-altitude days are too much. Add a small-group stargazing session in the evening if the sky and timing allow.

Plan B puts the extra money into the long day with many stops, where a private vehicle changes the most, instead of buying every tour privately. Our [three-day itinerary](/3-day-itinerary/) sets out the order of trips in more detail.

## Our verdict on private vs group tours

**Start with shared tours, then upgrade one or two.** Valle de la Luna, Laguna Cejar and stargazing work well in a well-run group. A private tour is easier to justify for a long Piedras Rojas day, a photography-led itinerary, or a family or group large enough to split the cost. El Tatio sits between the two: a private guide can make the morning more comfortable and attentive, but it will not remove the early start, the cold or the altitude.

Compare what each listing includes and ignore the adjectives. Choose the tour whose route, pace, group size and total price match your trip, and check the dates in the calendar for your stay, because schedules change with the weather.
