## Choose the endpoint before the price

The selected White & Green Travel product travels from San Pedro through Bolivia’s highlands to Salar de Uyuni, then returns to San Pedro on day four. If you want to continue through Bolivia, a one-way itinerary is a different product and needs its own confirmation. A low headline price does not help if it returns you to the wrong country.

We have verified the return product shown here. We have not labelled it a three-day one-way tour or used a Bolivia-departure product as if it began in Chile. Confirm the endpoint, luggage arrangement and border transfer before comparing offers.

## How the four days are used

**Day one** begins with the border journey and visits highland lagoons, the Dalí Desert area, thermal springs and the Sol de Mañana geothermal area, before an overnight in the Bolivian highlands. The advertised route approaches 5,000 metres: this is not a gentle first excursion after a sea-level flight. The [altitude guide](/altitude/) explains how to build up to it.

**Day two** continues through rock formations and lagoon landscapes towards a salt-hostel overnight. Expect substantial driving and basic infrastructure between stops.

**Day three** is the Salar de Uyuni day, with salt-flat photographs, a route including Incahuasi when accessible, and stops around Colchani and the train cemetery. The listing then describes travel back towards the highlands rather than finishing your booking in Uyuni.

**Day four** is the return to San Pedro. Its advertised midday arrival is an estimate. Do not plan a tight international or domestic flight connection around it.

## What is—and is not—included

Transport, a driver, accommodation, meals and an oxygen bottle are listed. Bolivian national-park admission is excluded. Ask which overnight rooms are shared or private, whether bathrooms and showers are shared, and what bedding is supplied. “Accommodation included” says very little about comfort.

The guiding language is Spanish in the current listing. A driver who handles the expedition is not automatically an English-speaking naturalist guide. If language matters, establish it before paying rather than assuming it will be similar to your San Pedro day tours.

## Altitude deserves its own decision

This itinerary includes highland sleeping as well as day visits. That is different from returning to San Pedro after [Piedras Rojas](/piedras-rojas-tours/) or [El Tatio](/el-tatio-geysers-tours/). Take time to acclimatise, discuss relevant health conditions with a clinician and read the [altitude guide](/altitude/) before booking.

An oxygen bottle in the vehicle does not make a rapid ascent risk-free. Ask how the operator handles illness, route changes and evacuation. Check that any insurance you choose covers the actual activities, altitude and countries involved.

## Border documents and cash

Entry requirements depend on nationality and can change. Use official Chilean and Bolivian immigration or consular information for your passport, then confirm the operator’s document and border instructions. Do not treat another traveller’s visa experience as your own requirement.

Ask which payments need Bolivian cash, what the current reserve charges are, and where you can obtain money before leaving. Carry the water, warm clothing and personal supplies requested by the supplier; remote overnight stops do not provide the same services as central San Pedro.

## Rainy-season expectations

Water on the salt flat can create reflections, but it can also alter roads and access to places such as Incahuasi. A “mirror effect” photograph is not a promise for every date, and a dry-season itinerary may be changed when water levels rise. Ask what route substitutions apply rather than buying on a single image.

For a short Chile-only stay, [multi-day Atacama packages](/multi-day-tours/) are the simpler comparison. Add Uyuni because you want the expedition and have time for it, not because the salt-flat name appears beside other San Pedro tours.
