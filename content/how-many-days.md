## Our practical recommendation

For most first-time visitors choosing from the main tours, three full days is a good place to start. It gives you a lower-desert introduction, stargazing when the sky is clear, one lagoon and one big highland excursion. Four full days lets you split Piedras Rojas and El Tatio and keep a quieter day in between.

People acclimatise at different speeds, and the [altitude guide](/altitude/) shows how high each tour goes. Your arrival elevation, health, symptoms and the tours’ restrictions still matter. If you cannot take a highland excursion, a shorter lower-desert stay can still be worthwhile.

## Days versus nights

Write your expected arrival at the hotel and required airport pickup on paper before counting tours. Our guide on [how to get to San Pedro](/getting-there/) covers the routes in. A flight to Calama is followed by baggage, meeting your transfer and the road journey. On departure, your pickup is well before the flight itself.

For example, arriving Monday evening and leaving Thursday morning gives Tuesday and Wednesday as full days. Calling it a “four-day trip” does not create four available excursions. Use the [Calama transfer guide](/calama-airport-to-san-pedro-de-atacama/) to plan those bookends.

| Time in town | Sensible focus | Main compromise |
|---|---|---|
| One full day | Moon Valley, with astronomy if the schedule fits | Limited chance to recover from cloud or delays |
| Two full days | Lower-desert highlights and one lagoon outing | High-altitude trips may be unsuitable soon after arrival |
| Three full days | A varied first visit with one highland priority | Choose between the longest headline excursions |
| Four full days | More space for both Piedras Rojas and El Tatio | Still requires suitability and access checks |
| Five or more | Slower pace, specialist interests or spare days | More nights and potentially more transport costs |

## What three days buys you

The [three-day plan](/3-day-itinerary/) begins with Moon Valley, adds a lagoon and stargazing when timings allow, then leaves the highland choice until later. It does not require booking every optional activity. A free afternoon may be the right choice after a long journey.

If you only want sunsets and astronomy, there is no need to buy a full-day highland excursion just to fill the schedule. If the plateau landscapes are the entire reason for visiting, prioritise those and give yourself more time around them.

## Why we like four full days for the core highlights

Piedras Rojas and El Tatio differ enough to justify both for a landscape-focused traveller. Their logistics also differ: one is a roughly ten-hour day, the other starts before dawn. An extra day makes it easier to separate them and avoid a late astronomy night immediately before the earliest pickup.

See the [four-day itinerary](/4-day-itinerary/) for the suggested order. More time does not guarantee perfect weather, but it gives you more choices when a route changes or you want to rest.

## Packages do not solve missing days

A three-day excursion bundle still needs three appropriate departure days. It may combine two outings on one date, but the supplier controls the order. Ask for the expected schedule before buying flights or assuming a package starts after your arrival transfer.

The [multi-day comparison](/multi-day-tours/) separates excursion bundles from accommodation and transfers. Add the extras when budgeting; a tour-package duration is not an all-inclusive holiday length.

## When to add more time

Add time for an unhurried stay, specialised astronomy interests, extra rest or a separate [Uyuni expedition](/uyuni-tours-from-san-pedro-de-atacama/). To fill the extra days, see our guide to [things to do in San Pedro de Atacama](/things-to-do/). The four-day Uyuni return product requires its own block of days and highland preparation. It cannot be tucked into the spare afternoon of a Chile itinerary.

If your dates are fixed, choose the strongest two or three experiences and protect the gaps between them. Cutting an activity is often the practical way to keep the trip enjoyable, especially when arrival timing or altitude response is uncertain. The [altitude guide](/altitude/) suggests a low-to-high order, and a central base from the [where-to-stay guide](/where-to-stay/) keeps early pickups simple.

## The rules behind every plan

1. **Climb gradually.** San Pedro sits at about 2,400 m. The Laguna Cejar operators themselves ask visitors to spend at least a day in town first, skip alcohol the day before and drink plenty of water. El Tatio and Piedras Rojas, at about 4,200–4,300 m, always come last.
2. **Never put stargazing the night before El Tatio.** Sky tours finish between about 22:00 and midnight, and El Tatio pickups run from about 04:30 to 05:10.
3. **Pair a morning tour with an afternoon tour, and avoid two full days back to back.** Rainbow Valley in the morning with Laguna Cejar in the afternoon, and El Tatio with an afternoon at Puritama, are the two classic doubles.
4. **Keep El Tatio off your departure day** unless your flight leaves Calama late in the afternoon.
5. **Check the closures** (Valle de Marte is reported to close on Mondays; Valle de la Luna closes on announced staff-training days) before you fix the schedule.

## How your flight shapes day one

- **Getting there:** nonstop flights from Santiago to Calama take a little over two hours, and the shuttle from Calama airport to San Pedro takes about an hour and a half. Our [Calama transfer guide](/calama-airport-to-san-pedro-de-atacama/) compares the shared and private options.
- **Morning arrival (landing by about 11:00):** you reach San Pedro by early afternoon, with time for lunch and a 15:00 Valle de la Luna pickup. Day one counts as a full day.
- **Afternoon arrival (landing after about 13:00):** treat day one as rest. Walk the plaza, eat early and sleep, then add a day to whichever plan you pick or start with its day-two schedule.
- **Departure day:** book your shuttle so you reach Calama at least two hours before your flight; with the drive, that means leaving San Pedro three and a half to four hours before take-off. A morning Rainbow Valley tour, back around 12:30, can fit before an evening flight. El Tatio is tiring and best avoided on that day.
- **Leaving for Uyuni:** cross-border tours pick up early in the morning, so make your last San Pedro night an early one, with no late stargazing. Our [Uyuni guide](/uyuni-tours-from-san-pedro-de-atacama/) covers the crossing.

## The five-day plan, with a buffer

**Best for:** photographers, families, travellers sensitive to altitude, and anyone visiting from June to September (snow) or January to March (summer storms in the highlands).

- **Day 1:** arrive and rest; Valle de la Luna from about 15:00 to 20:00.
- **Day 2:** a morning bike ride or walk to Pukará de Quitor and the Valle de Marte viewpoints (not on a Monday); lunch; Laguna Cejar from about 14:00 to 19:30.
- **Day 3:** Rainbow Valley from about 07:00 to 12:30; a free afternoon in town; stargazing in the evening.
- **Day 4:** Piedras Rojas and the highland lagoons, from about 07:00 to 18:00.
- **Day 5:** El Tatio from about 04:30 to 12:30, Puritama in the afternoon, then a farewell dinner.
- **Buffer:** if weather cancels day 4 or 5, use the free half of day 2 or 3 to reschedule. If nothing is cancelled, add sandboarding in Valle de Marte or the Salt Flats Route (below).

### With the Uyuni crossing (four days plus three)

Follow the four-day plan, then leave on day five for the three-day 4x4 tour to the Salar de Uyuni, or take the {{< gyg-link tour="774308" >}}4-Day Uyuni Salt Flats Tour (from about US$352, 4.5/5 from 88 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} if you are flying home from Calama. You cross at Hito Cajón, about 4,480 m, and sleep well above 4,000 m on the first night, so El Tatio and Piedras Rojas serve as your altitude training. In 2026, Bolivia's diesel shortage and the floating boliviano have made prices and departures less predictable, and Hito Cajón closes in snow, especially from June to September. Do not book a same-day onward flight after the crossing, and get the inclusions confirmed in writing. The [Uyuni guide](/uyuni-tours-from-san-pedro-de-atacama/) has the details.

### The self-drive version

Rent at Calama airport, where the rental desks are. A two-wheel-drive car works on the paved roads, but higher clearance helps on gravel roads such as those to El Tatio and Baltinache.

- **Day 1:** drive from Calama to San Pedro (about 100 km, roughly 1 h 15 min to 1 h 30 min). Visit Valle de la Luna in the late afternoon; buy the date-specific ticket online first. Vehicle entry runs 09:00–17:00 from September to March and 08:00–16:00 from April to August.
- **Day 2:** Valle de Marte in the morning (not on a Monday) and Pukará de Quitor; Laguna Cejar in the afternoon on a pre-booked timed ticket.
- **Day 3:** Piedras Rojas and the highland lagoons, heading south via Toconao and Socaire. Start early, carry water and fill the tank in San Pedro.
- **Day 4:** El Tatio. We recommend a guided tour for this one: it is about two hours on gravel in the dark, up to 4,300 m, before sunrise. If you drive, arrive by about 06:30–07:00; visitors who arrive around 11:00 report much less steam. The park opens 06:00–16:00.

### The all-inclusive lodge version

All-inclusive lodges such as {{< hotel-link "explora" >}}explora Atacama{{< /hotel-link >}} (three-night minimum), {{< hotel-link "nayara" >}}Nayara Alto Atacama{{< /hotel-link >}} and {{< hotel-link "tierra_atacama" >}}Tierra Atacama{{< /hotel-link >}} bundle meals, the bar and guided excursions, and explora and Nayara run sessions at their own observatories. A four-night stay lets you follow the four-day plan in private or small-group vehicles, often with later El Tatio departures. Our [where to stay guide](/where-to-stay/) compares the lodges.

### The budget version

- Take the shared airport shuttle or a public bus via Calama.
- Visit Valle de la Luna, Valle de Marte and Pukará de Quitor on a rented bicycle. Carry three litres of water or more, and ride early or late.
- Book group tours with agencies in town or online, and pay the park entries yourself.
- If you must cut, drop Puritama and the highland lagoons; keep El Tatio and Cejar.

## Combining tours on the same day

| Combination | Works? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rainbow Valley (morning) and Laguna Cejar (afternoon) | ✅ Yes | Back by about 12:30, pickup around 14:00 |
| El Tatio (morning) and Puritama (afternoon) | ✅ Yes | El Tatio returns around 12:30, leaving the afternoon slot free; a good way to recover |
| Valle de la Luna (sunset) and stargazing | ⚠️ Possible | Valle de la Luna returns around 20:00; pick a later slot; tiring on arrival day |
| Stargazing the night before El Tatio | ❌ No | Back around midnight, pickup from 04:30 |
| Highland lagoons and stargazing the same night | ⚠️ Only if the next day is free | You have just spent an 11-hour day at about 4,200 m |
| El Tatio on your arrival day or day two | ❌ No | Too high too soon |
| Piedras Rojas and El Tatio on consecutive days | ✅ Yes, late in the trip | Both are high, but you are better adapted by then |

**Moon phase:** the darkest skies come in the week around the new moon. Around the full moon, many operators switch to moon-and-planets sessions, and some suspend standard tours on the brightest nights. If stargazing is a priority, check the lunar calendar before you fix your dates and put it on the darkest night of your stay. Our [stargazing guide](/stargazing-tours/) compares the operators.

**Closures to check:**

- **Valle de Marte:** reported to close every Monday.
- **Valle de la Luna:** closes on announced staff-training days (in October 2026, the 6th, 13th, 19th, 20th and 27th) and after rain or bad weather. The [Valle de la Luna guide](/moon-valley-tours/) tracks the dates.
- **Laguna Cejar:** we found no fixed weekly closing day in 2026. Tickets are timed and date-specific, and the site can close in extreme weather, in which case you are rescheduled by email.

## Plans for different travellers

**Families with children**

- El Tatio: the park does not admit children aged 0–8, and some agencies set their own minimum of 12. Check before booking.
- Laguna Piedra, the swimming lagoon at Cejar, is not suitable for children under 6; children up to 12 pay CLP 5,000.
- Valle de la Luna: young children enter free; check the official site for the reduced rates.
- Puritama: children pay a reduced rate, and operators set a minimum age of about 5.
- **A family four-day plan:** Valle de la Luna, then Cejar, then Valle de Marte for easy dune play plus stargazing, then the highland lagoons if the children are over about 6 and coping well with the altitude.

**Older and altitude-sensitive travellers**

- The El Tatio park does not recommend the visit for people over 70 and does not admit pregnant visitors.
- A low-altitude plan, all at about 2,300–2,500 m: Valle de la Luna, Laguna Cejar and Tebinquiche, Valle de Marte, Pukará de Quitor, Aldea de Tulor, Laguna Chaxa and stargazing.
- Cejar charges CLP 10,000 from age 60, and other sites have senior rates.
- For symptoms and prevention, read our [altitude guide](/altitude/).

**Photographers**

- Plan sunrise at El Tatio, where the steam is strongest in the cold, roughly 06:00–07:00, and late light at Tebinquiche.
- Book an astrophotography tour around the new moon.
- The five-day plan gives you a second try if the light fails.

**Adventure travellers**

- Sandboarding in Valle de Marte, cycling the valleys and the Salt Flats Route.
- High summits need full acclimatisation and a specialist operator, so schedule them after El Tatio. ## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Doing El Tatio on day one or two.** Operators and the Cejar site advise at least a day in town first; El Tatio is about 1,900 m higher than San Pedro.
2. **Booking stargazing the night before a 04:30 pickup.** You get back around midnight and sleep through the geysers.
3. **Arriving at El Tatio late.** Self-drivers who arrive around 11:00 report little visible steam.
4. **Expecting to linger at Laguna Cejar.** You get about an hour on site and should spend no more than about 20 minutes in the water. Go for the float and the flamingos, not a beach afternoon.
5. **Wearing sunscreen into the lagoon.** It is not allowed before swimming; put it on afterwards.
6. **Not buying Valle de la Luna tickets online.** Tickets are sold online only, for a set date.
7. **Running out of cash.** Several entry fees are cash only, and tours often collect them on the day.
8. **Leaving no buffer in winter.** Snow closes the high roads, and one cancelled day can wipe out El Tatio or Piedras Rojas on a three-day trip.
9. **Assuming Baltinache, ALMA or Salar de Tara are open.** In late 2026 they are not, or not for swimming.

## Booking checklist

- Flights land in Calama by about 11:00 on day one, or you add a night.
- The airport shuttle is booked both ways.
- The Valle de la Luna ticket is bought online for the exact date, after checking the training-day closures.
- The Laguna Cejar timed ticket is booked, through an agency or the official site.
- The Puritama entry is bought online for the morning or afternoon slot.
- El Tatio sits on day three or later, and not after a stargazing night.
- Stargazing is on the darkest night available.
- Valle de Marte is not on a Monday.
- You have Chilean pesos for the cash fees.
- You have layers for dawns below freezing and warm afternoons, sunglasses, a reusable bottle, swimwear and a towel.
- High-altitude tours have free cancellation in case of weather.
- Your travel insurance covers altitude.
