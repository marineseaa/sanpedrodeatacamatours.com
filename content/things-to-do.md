## The 15 best things to do in San Pedro de Atacama

### 1. Valle de la Luna (Moon Valley)

**Why go:** you walk among salt-crusted ridges, caves, the Tres Marías rock formations and a giant dune, all just west of town. It is the easiest big landscape in the Atacama and a good first-day trip, because the standard tour takes about four hours and leaves part of the day free.

- **Duration:** a 4–5 h tour. CONAF's official trails run from the 80 m Tres Marías walk to the 3 km, three-hour Achache route.
- **Price:** the {{< gyg-link tour="466455" >}}Valle de la Luna Shared Sightseeing Tour (4.7/5 from 362 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} starts at about US$32 and leaves the CLP 10,800 reserve ticket to you. The {{< gyg-link tour="471706" >}}Valle de la Luna tour with entry and cocktail (4.5/5 from 441 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} starts at about US$63 and includes the ticket and hotel transport. Add the missing ticket before you compare the two. Reduced rates exist for students, teenagers and seniors; check them on the official shop.
- **Best time:** afternoon into sunset. In windy seasons the reserve has recommended morning visits.
- **2026 status:** the official Valle de la Luna site says the **Mirador de Ckari** sector has been closed until further notice since 27 May 2026 while its safety is assessed after an earthquake. The operator sanpedroatacama.com says it may stay shut possibly until the end of 2026, partly because of widening works on the Bioceanic Route. Tours now watch sunset from other authorised spots. The official site also lists staff-training closures on 6, 13, 19, 20 and 27 October, so check it before you go.
- **Tour or DIY:** both work. You can bike or drive in, and you must buy your ticket online in advance. Cyclists enter between 09:00 and 11:00 from September to March.
- **Full details:** [Valle de la Luna tours](/moon-valley-tours/)

### 2. Stargazing and astrophotography

**Why go:** high altitude, very dry air and little light pollution make this one of the world's great places to see the night sky. Through a telescope you can see the core of the Milky Way, the Magellanic Clouds and Saturn's rings.

- **Duration:** about 2.5 h, starting after dark.
- **Price:** the {{< gyg-link tour="542449" >}}San Pedro de Atacama Stargazing Experience (4.7/5 from 805 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} starts at about US$30. The {{< gyg-link tour="209091" >}}Stargazing and Andean Astronomy with Wine (4.7/5 from 797 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} costs about the same and adds an Andean reading of the sky. Both combine a guided explanation, telescope use and a souvenir photo; neither is an ALMA research-observatory visit.
- **Best time:** around the new moon. The Moon, cloud and the actual observing time all affect what you see. Nights are cold all year, so dress warmly.
- **Tip:** do not book your first night if you arrive tired from Calama. Pick a clear, moonless night mid-trip and leave another free evening in case the session is moved. For month-level advice, see our [best-time guide](/best-time-to-visit/); conditions on a particular night can still differ.
- **Full details:** [Atacama stargazing tours](/stargazing-tours/)

### 3. El Tatio geysers at sunrise

**Why go:** according to geyser researchers Glennon and Pfaff (2003), cited by Oregon State University's Volcano World, "with over 80 active geysers, El Tatio is the largest geyser field in the southern hemisphere and the third largest field in the world." The geysers steam hardest in the freezing dawn. In Kunza, the Lickanantay language, *Tatio* means "the grandfather who cries".

- **Duration:** about 6 h, with pickup between 04:30 and 05:10.
- **Price:** the {{< gyg-link tour="345946" >}}Tatio Geysers at Sunrise tour with breakfast (4.7/5 from 1,148 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} starts at about US$73 and adds Andean wildlife scenery on the way back. Entry is CLP 15,000, paid separately, and children are admitted from age 9 under the park's current rules.
- **Altitude:** about 4,320 m. Do it on day 3 or later and read the age and health restrictions before you book.
- **Watch out:** temperatures well below zero at dawn, and snow closures in winter.
- If the altitude or the departure time rules it out, use the day for a lower desert outing or rest.
- **Full details:** [El Tatio geyser tours](/el-tatio-geysers-tours/)

### 4. Altiplanic lagoons (Miscanti and Miñiques) + Piedras Rojas

**Why go:** this is the Atacama's postcard. Cobalt lagoons sit under snow-capped volcanoes at more than 4,000 m, and the rust-red rocks of Piedras Rojas lie beside the Salar de Aguas Calientes. Expect vicuñas, the Tropic of Capricorn marker and the village of Socaire.

- **Duration:** a full day of about 10 h, roughly 07:00 to 17:00.
- **Price:** the {{< gyg-link tour="190853" >}}Piedras Rojas, Altiplanic Lagoons and Salar full-day tour (4.7/5 from 828 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} starts at about US$86, with admission extra. The {{< gyg-link tour="632997" >}}Triple tour of the Red Stones, highland lagoons and Chaxa (4.7/5 from 325 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} costs about the same and ends at the flamingo lagoon.
- **Altitude:** up to about 4,200 m, with a short walk at Piedras Rojas.
- **Note:** Salar de Tara has been closed to tourism for years, so do not let an old blog sell it to you. Check each stop by name, because "lagoons" in a title does not tell you which lakes and reserves are included.
- **Full details:** [Piedras Rojas tours](/piedras-rojas-tours/) and the [best time to visit](/best-time-to-visit/) for the snow months

### 5. Laguna Cejar, Ojos del Salar and a Tebinquiche sunset

**Why go:** you float in very salty water inside the Salar de Atacama, then toast the sunset on the mirror-like salt crust of Laguna Tebinquiche.

- **Duration:** about 5 h (afternoon).
- **Price:** Cejar entry is CLP 15,000 for ages 13–59, CLP 10,000 from 60 and CLP 5,000 under 12; Tebinquiche charges a small extra fee. The {{< viator-link path="San-Pedro-de-Atacama/San-Pedro-de-Atacama-Cejar-Lagoon/d5499-422580P12" >}}Laguna Cejar floating tour on Viator (5.0/5 from 80 reviews){{< /viator-link >}} is the best-reviewed listing we found; the GetYourGuide Cejar tour has only a handful of reviews so far.
- **Swimming rules:** you float in **Laguna Piedra**, part of the Cejar complex, not in Cejar itself. Laguna Piedra is not suitable for children under 6. The site has showers. Bring flip-flops, because the salt crust cuts feet.
- **Best for:** your first or second day, since it is low and relaxing.
- **Full details:** [Laguna Cejar tours](/laguna-cejar-tours/) and the [Baltinache versus Cejar comparison](/baltinache-vs-laguna-cejar/)

### 6. Laguna Chaxa: flamingos on the salt flat

**Why go:** Chaxa is among the most reliable places to see flamingos close to San Pedro. It lies in the flamingo sector of the Reserva Nacional Los Flamencos, in the middle of the Salar de Atacama.

- **Duration:** about 1 h on site, usually combined with the lagoons tour or with Toconao.
- **Price:** a community entry fee, paid separately; the figures we found disagree, so check the current rate at the gate or with your operator. The {{< gyg-link tour="632997" >}}Red Stones, highland lagoons and Chaxa triple tour{{< /gyg-link >}} includes the stop.
- **Best time:** late afternoon light, or sunset as the last stop on triple tours.

### 7. Termas de Puritama hot springs

**Why go:** terraced thermal pools sit in a canyon fed by the Puritama River, with water at 28–31 °C according to the official site. It is a different kind of water visit from Cejar, warm soaking instead of salt-water floating, and a good way to spend a rest day or recover after El Tatio.

- **Duration:** half day, in the official AM slot (09:30–13:30) or PM slot (14:30–18:00).
- **Price:** book entry on the official site, which sets the price by date; children under 12 pay a children's rate and under-3s go free. Transfers are sold separately, and the selected GetYourGuide transfer excludes admission.
- **Location:** about 3,500 m, 28 km north of town on Route B-245.
- **Booking:** buy online and bring your ticket and ID, because there is no phone signal. No food is sold, so bring your own. Ask about paths and pool access if mobility affects your choice.
- **Full details:** [Puritama hot springs](/puritama-hot-springs-tours/)

### 8. Rainbow Valley (Valle del Arcoíris) + Yerbas Buenas petroglyphs

**Why go:** the hills are striped red, green and white by minerals. On the way you stop at Yerbas Buenas, one of the most important rock-art sites in the region, with llamas, foxes and caravan figures carved by pre-Hispanic travellers.

- **Duration:** 5–6 h (morning).
- **Price:** GetYourGuide has no listing; the {{< viator-link path="San-Pedro-de-Atacama/Rainbow-Valley-Tour-from-San-Pedro-de-Atacama/d5499-6315CJCRAIN" >}}Rainbow Valley Tour from San Pedro on Viator (4.7/5 from 35 reviews){{< /viator-link >}} is the one we would book. Two small entry fees are paid in cash.
- **Altitude:** about 3,200–3,500 m. Operators recommend it in your first days as a step up before the 4,000 m trips.
- **Best for:** an easy, cultural warm-up.

### 9. Sandboarding in Valle de la Muerte (Valle de Marte)

**Why go:** this is the Atacama's adventure classic. You ride steep sand slopes a few minutes from town, with the Licancabur volcano in view.

- **Duration:** 3–4 h, as a morning or sunset session.
- **Price:** board, boots and a short lesson are usually included in the operator's price; the valley charges its own entry. We found no online listing with enough reviews to recommend, so compare agencies in town.
- **Tip:** you walk back up after every run, and at 2,500 m that is harder than you expect. Bring water, and check the day before, because the valley is reported to close on Mondays.

### 10. Biking: Garganta del Diablo and the Catarpe Valley

**Why go:** this is the cheapest half-day adventure in town. You ride north along the San Pedro river valley into the narrow Devil's Throat canyon, then return past Pukará de Quitor.

- **Duration:** 2–4 h.
- **Price:** bikes are rented from shops on Caracoles and Toconao streets, so compare daily rates; the canyon charges a small community entry fee. If you prefer a guide, the {{< viator-link path="San-Pedro-de-Atacama/4-Hours-riding-a-bicycle-in-San-Pedro-de-Atacama/d5499-409609P1" >}}Devil's Throat bike tour on Viator (5.0/5 from 111 reviews){{< /viator-link >}} starts at about US$95.
- **Options:** e-bikes are widely offered. You can also cycle to Valle de la Luna, about 45 minutes each way on the flat, but cyclists must enter by 11:00.
- **Tip:** start early, because the afternoon wind and sun are hard going. Carry 2 litres of water and a repair kit.

### 11. Pukará de Quitor and Aldea de Tulor (archaeology)

**Why go:** Pukará de Quitor is a 12th-century stone fortress on a hillside above the San Pedro river. It was one of the last strongholds against the Spanish in 1540 and was declared a National Monument in 1982. Aldea de Tulor is a village of round mud houses more than 2,000 years old.

- **Duration:** 2 h on your own (Quitor is a 3 km walk or ride north of town) or a 4–5 h combined tour.
- **Price:** each site charges a small entry fee in cash. Agencies in town sell combined tours; the online listings we found have only a couple of reviews each.
- **Note:** some visitors find Tulor underwhelming because much of what you see is reconstruction. A good guide makes the difference.

### 12. Toconao village and Ayllu high-altitude wine

**Why go:** Toconao is a white volcanic-stone village 38 km south of town at about 2,500 m. Its church bell tower dates from 1750 and is a National Monument. Nearby, the green Quebrada de Jerez ravine is full of quince and fruit orchards. Toconao is also home to **Ayllu**, wine made by the Cooperativa Viñateros de Altura Lickan Antay, whose 18 farming families grow vines above 2,400 m, some on terraces at Socaire above 3,500 m. *Ayllu* means "community" in Kunza.

- **Duration:** half day. Toconao is also a stop on many lagoon tours.
- **Price:** the cooperative runs cellar and vineyard tastings; check its current prices when you book.
- **Best for:** food and wine lovers, culture, a rest day.

### 13. The Uyuni salt flat crossing into Bolivia

**Why go:** this is one of South America's great overland trips. You cross coloured lagoons, geysers and flamingo-filled salt flats to reach the Salar de Uyuni.

- **Duration:** 3 days and 2 nights one way, or 4 days for the return, crossing at the **Hito Cajón** border (about 4,480 m).
- **Price:** the {{< gyg-link tour="774308" >}}4-Day Uyuni Salt Flats Tour from San Pedro de Atacama (4.6/5 from 86 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} is a return trip from about US$352. For a one-way crossing, the {{< viator-link path="San-Pedro-de-Atacama/Uyuni-Saltflat-from-San-Pedro-de-Atacama/d5499-86163P17" >}}Uyuni salt flat tour on Viator, round trip or one way (4.4/5 from 46 reviews){{< /viator-link >}} starts at about US$412. The Eduardo Avaroa reserve fee is paid in cash and is usually not included.
- **Before you go:** snow can close Hito Cajón and the Eduardo Avaroa reserve in winter, as it did in 2026. Bolivia's fuel supply has also been unreliable, so ask your operator how it is running its vehicles. Bolivia is one hour behind Chile during Chilean summer time.
- The trip needs its own block of days and remote highland overnights, so don't try to fit it between a sunset and an airport transfer.
- **Full details:** [Uyuni tours from San Pedro de Atacama](/uyuni-tours-from-san-pedro-de-atacama/)

### 14. Volcano climbs: Cerro Toco, Láscar and more

**Why go:** you stand above 5,000 m with views across the Andes. Cerro Toco (about 5,600 m) is the usual first 5,000 m peak, because a vehicle takes you close to the top. Láscar is one of Chile's most active volcanoes, and summits there depend on its activity status.

- **Duration:** full day.
- **Price:** the {{< viator-link path="San-Pedro-de-Atacama/Ascent-of-Toco-Volcano/d5499-383623P1" >}}Ascent of Toco Volcano on Viator (5.0/5 from 56 reviews){{< /viator-link >}} starts at about US$110. Private ascents cost more.
- **Rule:** attempt it only after four or five days at altitude. Read our [altitude guide](/altitude/) first.

### 15. Hot-air balloon at sunrise

**Why go:** you drift over the Salar de Atacama and the Cordillera de la Sal as the sun rises behind Licancabur.

- **Duration:** about 4 h in total, about 1 h in the air, with pickup around 05:00.
- **Price:** among the most expensive outings in town; the local agency Nomades listed it at CLP 280,000 per person in 2026, and private baskets cost far more. We found no online listing to recommend.
- **Note:** flights depend on the weather and are cancelled in strong wind, so book with a cancellation guarantee.

### Honourable mentions

- **Lagunas Escondidas de Baltinache:** seven turquoise salt lagoons about an hour away. **Swimming has been suspended since 29 September 2026.** SanPedroAtacama.com says bathing and floating are off until the end of November 2026, with one lagoon expected to reopen for floating in December. The site stays open for viewing, walks and picnics, and the {{< gyg-link tour="645617" >}}Baltinache Hidden Lagoons tour (4.5/5 from 146 reviews){{< /gyg-link >}} still runs as a sightseeing trip. Go for the scenery, not the swim. See [Baltinache tours](/baltinache-tours/).
- **Horseback riding:** half-day rides from local ranches into the Cordillera de la Sal and the Catarpe valley.
- **ALMA Observatory:** the world's largest radio telescope. ALMA says its public visit programme is currently suspended, with a limited programme for educational, research and media groups. It held special public open days in October 2026; watch almaobservatory.org for news of the regular programme.
- **Vallecito and Cordillera de la Sal hikes and photography tours:** quieter alternatives to the Valle de la Luna crowds.

## In town: things to see in San Pedro de Atacama

Keep a few unbooked hours around the centre for a meal, a quiet walk and the practical tasks that make the excursions easier.

- **Iglesia de San Pedro:** this whitewashed adobe church stands on the west side of the plaza and is the largest of the Atacameño churches. Its walls are almost a metre thick. Look up at the ceiling, made of **cactus-wood (cardón) slats** covered with mud and straw and tied with llama-leather straps instead of nails. Inside are murals dated to 1774; the tower was added in 1964. It was declared a National Monument in 1951. Entry is free (donations welcome), it usually opens in the daytime with a midday break, and Sunday mass is a lovely local moment.
- **Plaza de Armas and Calle Caracoles:** pepper trees, benches and the town's social life. Caracoles is the main street of restaurants, agencies and shops, pedestrianised in the centre.
- **Paseo Artesanal (craft market):** alpaca and llama-wool knits, ceramics and Lickanantay crafts beside the plaza. Prices are often better in Toconao and from makers who sell directly.
- **Museo Gustavo Le Paige: closed.** The original museum, with about 380,000 artefacts covering more than 11,000 years of Atacameño history, was dismantled between 2015 and 2017. The firm that won the 2015 tender for a new building later sued, arguing that the new museum did not fit on the site (BioBioChile, 2017). In April 2022 the Ministry of Public Works awarded a new pre-feasibility study (La Tercera), and the new museum still has no opening date. Ignore websites that list opening hours unless the Universidad Católica del Norte confirms them. The ancestral human remains are no longer displayed, out of respect for the Atacameño communities.
- **Tourist information office:** on Toconao street, open on weekdays and Saturday mornings; check the hours locally.

Choose [central accommodation](/where-to-stay/) if you want to walk to agencies and dinner.

## Food and drink in San Pedro de Atacama

### Dishes and flavours to try

- **Patasca:** a hearty Andean stew of mote (hominy) and meat.
- **Llama meat:** lean and slightly sweet, served grilled, as carpaccio or in a stew.
- **Quinoa:** in soups, risottos and salads.
- **Chañar and algarrobo:** native desert-tree fruits used in syrups, flours and desserts.
- **Rica-rica:** an aromatic desert herb found in ice cream, pisco sours and even local beer.
- **Sopaipillas:** fried pumpkin flatbreads, good with a craft beer.
- **Ayllu wine:** Toconao's high-altitude Syrah, Malbec, Pinot Noir and Moscatel.

### Nightlife: what to do at night in San Pedro de Atacama

Nights in San Pedro are quiet on purpose: dinner by the fire, live Andean music, a craft beer and the stars. There are no clubs.

- **Closing times:** venues selling alcohol close early by big-city standards, usually before midnight on weeknights and a little later at weekends. Check current municipal hours locally.
- **Order food with drinks:** most venues hold restaurant licences, so you order food with alcohol. **Chela Cabur** is the well-known beer bar with loud rock, and **Cervecería St. Peter** brews local beers, including one with rica-rica.
- **Drinking in the street or plaza is illegal**, and the Carabineros (police) fine people for it.
- **The best late-night activity is a stargazing tour.** Alcohol, altitude and a 04:30 El Tatio pickup are a bad combination.

## Free and cheap things to do in San Pedro de Atacama

1. Walk the **plaza, church and Caracoles** (free).
2. Watch sunset from the **edge of town** or the road towards Valle de la Muerte (free; respect private and community land).
3. **Bike to Garganta del Diablo and Catarpe** (bike rental plus a small entry fee).
4. **Walk to Pukará de Quitor** (about 3 km; small entry fee).
5. **Stargaze from your hostel courtyard** on a moonless night. Away from street lights you can see the Milky Way with the naked eye.
6. **Drive to Laguna Cejar or Valle de la Luna** with friends and split the car, so you pay only the entries.
7. Browse the **craft market** and go to **Sunday mass** at the church.

## Rest days and bad-weather alternatives

- **After El Tatio or on a rest day:** Puritama hot springs, a long lunch in town, the craft market, Quitor at golden hour, or yoga and spa treatments offered by several hotels and studios.
- **When snow closes the high sites** (most often in the winter months and during the summer Altiplanic rains): swap El Tatio or the lagoons for Valle de la Luna, Laguna Cejar, Rainbow Valley or sandboarding, which sit lower and are rarely affected. See the [best time to visit](/best-time-to-visit/).
- **When wind or dust is bad:** choose the archaeology sites, Toconao and the wine cellar, or a spa afternoon.

- **How many days?** Four full days cover the top five; five or six give you a buffer for weather and altitude. Add three or four days if you are crossing to Uyuni. ## Responsible travel in the Atacama

- **Many sites are run by Lickanantay communities,** among them Cejar, Tebinquiche, Rainbow Valley, Tulor, Quitor and the land around Puritama. Your entry fee goes to them. Stay on marked trails, because some land is sacred or ancestral and closed to visitors.
- **Do not climb on formations.** One of the Tres Marías has already been lost to damage. Keep off salt crusts and rock art.
- **No sunscreen in the lagoons.** Operators ban sunscreen and oils before floating at Laguna Piedra and, when it reopens, Baltinache. Showers are short because water is scarce.
- **Water is precious** in the driest non-polar desert on Earth. Take short showers and refill bottles instead of buying plastic.
- **Drones:** expect them to be banned or restricted in protected areas and community sites. Ask before you fly.
- **Leave no trace:** pack out everything, including fruit peel and toilet paper.
- **Respect closures** such as Ckari, Baltinache swimming and Salar de Tara. They exist for safety or to let the ecosystem recover.

## Common visitor regrets

1. **"We did El Tatio on day one and felt awful."** Save the 4,000 m trips for day 3 or later.
2. **"We didn't bring cash."** Many entries are cash only.
3. **"We crammed two tours into a day."** A 04:30 El Tatio start plus an afternoon tour is exhausting. Pair El Tatio with a rest afternoon or Puritama.
4. **"We booked stargazing during a full moon."** Check the lunar calendar first.
5. **"We under-packed for the cold."** Dawn at El Tatio is well below freezing, while midday is hot. Bring layers, a hat, gloves and SPF 50, and see [what to pack](/what-to-pack/).
6. **"We trusted an old blog."** Ckari, Baltinache swimming, Tara, ALMA and the museum have all changed.
